The map of mathematics

Mathematicians sort their research into the Mathematics Subject Classification: 63 areas, split into 534 sections. This page lists every section, says its key idea in one sentence, suggests an elementary way in for a curious kid, and links the sheets and demos where the idea is already planted.

The bet behind it: almost every part of mathematics has an intuitive example a 12-year-old can hold. Where we think that is hard, we say so.

06 Order, lattices, ordered algebraic structures 1 of 6
○ 06A Ordered sets

An order says when one thing comes before another, even if some pairs cannot be compared at all.

Natural fit Way in (grade 5): Draw the getting-dressed diagram: socks before shoes, underwear before trousers. Find every valid dressing order and notice some items are incomparable.

○ 06B Lattices

A lattice is an order where every two elements have a best common lower bound and a best common upper bound.

Natural fit Way in (grade 6): Draw the divisors of 12 in layers, with a line up when one divides the next. For any two, find their gcd below and their lcm above.

○ 06C Modular lattices, complemented lattices

Modular and complemented lattices are orders with extra balance rules, like those followed by subspaces in geometry.

Hard Way in (grade 11): List the subspaces of the plane: just the origin, every line through it, and the whole plane. Check that each line has a "complement" line, and many choices work.

○ 06D Distributive lattices

Distributive lattices are orders where meet and join distribute over each other, just like AND and OR do.

Stretch Way in (grade 8): Using divisors of 30 with gcd and lcm, check that gcd(a, lcm(b,c)) equals lcm(gcd(a,b), gcd(a,c)) for several triples. Then find a lattice drawing where it fails.

● 06E Boolean algebras (Boolean rings)

Boolean algebra is the algebra of true and false, or of subsets, and it runs every digital circuit.

Natural fit Way in (grade 6): Shade Venn diagrams to check that NOT (A or B) is the same as (NOT A) and (NOT B). Then build that rule from switches in a light circuit.

Ordered algebraic structures combine arithmetic with an order, asking which ordering rules fit together with adding and multiplying.

Natural fit Way in (grade 6): Toss 10 coins many times: 5 heads happens 252 ways out of 1024, all heads only once. With a million coins, near-half is almost certain. That is equilibrium.

Natural fit Way in (grade 6): Model a polymer as a random walk: flip a coin 16 times, step left or right, and measure how far the chain's end got. Typical distance is about 4, not 16.

Natural fit Way in (grade 7): Block-spin zoom: colour a 9x9 grid of random black and white cells, replace each 3x3 block with its majority colour, and repeat to see large-scale structure.
