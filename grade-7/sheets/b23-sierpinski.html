The triangle with no area

Remove the middle of a triangle, then the middle of each piece, for ever. What is left has no area and an endless edge.

Name ____________________Date ____________

Tonight's idea

Start with a solid triangle. Mark the middle of each side and join the three marks. That makes four equal smaller triangles. Remove the middle one.

Three triangles are left. Do the same to each of them, then to each of the nine that leaves, and never stop. The shape you are heading for is the Sierpinski triangle.

Area. Each step removes one quarter of every triangle and keeps three quarters. So the area is multiplied by \(\tfrac34\) at every step. Keep going and it drops below any positive number you name. In the end, no area is left.

Edge. Add up the sides of all the triangles that remain. Each step makes three times as many triangles, with sides half as long. So the total edge is multiplied by \(3 \times \tfrac12 = \tfrac32\) at every step. It grows for ever.

Each step: area \(\times\, \tfrac34\), total edge \(\times\, \tfrac32\).

Your turn

  1. Add a step 4 column to the table: the number of triangles, the side of each, the area left and the total edge.
  2. After which step is less than a quarter of the area left for the first time? Use decimals: \(\tfrac34 = 0.75\).
  3. Go to step 6 of the 16 cm triangle. (a) How many triangles are there? (b) How long is each side? (c) What is the total edge, in metres?
  4. Try it with a square. Cut the square into 9 equal small squares, 3 by 3, and remove the middle one. Repeat on each of the 8 that are left. (a) What is the area multiplied by at each step? (b) What fraction is left after 3 steps? (c) Does the area still head for zero? Say why.

Stretch: hiding in Pascal's triangle

Write out the first 8 rows of Pascal's triangle, down to the row 1, 7, 21, 35, 35, 21, 7, 1. Shade every odd number. What picture appears? How many of the 36 numbers are shaded?

Answers

  1. 81 triangles, each with 1 cm sides. Area left \(\tfrac{81}{256}\). Total edge \(162 \times \tfrac32 = 243\) cm, which is also \(81 \times 3\).
  2. Step 5. The area goes 0.75, 0.5625, 0.42, 0.32, 0.24 (rounded). Step 4 leaves about 0.32, still more than a quarter. Step 5 leaves about 0.24.
  3. (a) \(3^6 = 729\). (b) Halve 16 six times: 0.25 cm. (c) \(729 \times 3 \times 0.25 = 546.75\) cm, about 5.47 m. All of it fits inside a triangle you could cover with your hand.
  4. (a) 8 of every 9 small squares are kept, so \(\tfrac89\). (b) \(\tfrac89 \times \tfrac89 \times \tfrac89 = \tfrac{512}{729}\), about 0.70. (c) Yes. Every step keeps only \(\tfrac89\) of what was there, so the area keeps shrinking and never settles above zero. It is just slower: after 20 steps about 0.09 is left. This shape is called the Sierpinski carpet.

Stretch. The odd numbers make the pattern of the Sierpinski triangle, with the even numbers as the holes. The rows contain 1, 2, 2, 4, 2, 4, 4 and 8 odd numbers, so 27 of the 36 are shaded. That is \(3 \times 3 \times 3\): nine little triangles of three odd numbers each.
