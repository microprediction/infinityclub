Angle chasing

Angles on a line make 180°, angles at a point make 360°, opposite angles at a crossing are equal. Three facts unlock a whole diagram.

Name ____________________Date ____________

Tonight's idea

An angle measures a turn. A full turn, all the way round, is 360° (° means degrees). Half a turn leaves you facing the opposite way, along a straight line, so a straight line is 180°. Two facts follow.

On a line: angles that sit side by side on a straight line add to 180°. At a point: angles that go all the way round a point add to 360°. At a crossing: when two straight lines cross, opposite angles are equal.

The third fact comes from the first. In the crossing, \(a\) and \(b\) sit on one straight line, so \(a + b = 180^\circ\). But \(b\) and \(c\) sit on the other straight line, so \(b + c = 180^\circ\) too. Both \(a\) and \(c\) are "180° take away \(b\)", so they are equal.

Angle chasing means starting from the angles you know and using these facts, one step at a time, until every angle is found. Give a reason for each step.

Watch one: two lines cross

In the crossing above, suppose \(a = x + 20^\circ\) and \(b = 3x\). Find all four angles.

On a line: \(x + 20^\circ + 3x = 180^\circ\). So \(4x = 160^\circ\) and \(x = 40^\circ\).

Then \(a = 60^\circ\) and \(b = 120^\circ\).

At a crossing: \(c = a = 60^\circ\) and \(d = b = 120^\circ\).

Check at a point: \(60 + 120 + 60 + 120 = 360\). It fits.

Your turn

  1. Two straight lines cross. One of the four angles is 72°. Find the other three, with a reason for each.
  2. (a) Three angles side by side on a straight line are 40°, 85° and \(x\). Find \(x\). (b) Four angles go all the way round a point: 90°, 110°, 75° and \(y\). Find \(y\).
  3. Three angles side by side on a straight line are \(x\), \(2x\) and \(3x\). Find \(x\). What is special about the largest angle?
  4. Three straight lines cross at one point and make six angles. Two of them are 35° and 80°. Find \(p\), \(q\), \(r\) and \(s\). Say which fact you used each time.

Stretch: halve them both

A ray stands on a straight line and makes two angles, \(a\) and \(b\). Draw a line that cuts \(a\) exactly in half, and another that cuts \(b\) exactly in half. Show that these two new lines always meet at a right angle, whatever \(a\) and \(b\) are.

Answers

  1. The opposite angle is 72° (at a crossing). Each neighbour is \(180^\circ - 72^\circ = 108^\circ\) (on a line). Check: \(72 + 108 + 72 + 108 = 360\).
  2. (a) \(x = 180^\circ - 40^\circ - 85^\circ = 55^\circ\). (b) \(y = 360^\circ - 90^\circ - 110^\circ - 75^\circ = 85^\circ\).
  3. \(x + 2x + 3x = 6x = 180^\circ\), so \(x = 30^\circ\). The angles are 30°, 60° and 90°. The largest is a right angle.
  4. 35°, 80° and \(p\) sit side by side on the horizontal line, so \(p = 180^\circ - 35^\circ - 80^\circ = 65^\circ\) (on a line). Then \(q = 35^\circ\), \(r = 80^\circ\) and \(s = 65^\circ\), each equal to the angle opposite it (at a crossing). Check: \(35 + 80 + 65 + 35 + 80 + 65 = 360\).

Stretch. On a line, \(a + b = 180^\circ\). The angle between the two new lines is half of \(a\) plus half of \(b\). That is half of \(a + b\), which is half of 180°, so it is 90° every time.
