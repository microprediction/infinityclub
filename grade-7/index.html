∞ Grade 7

For curious 12 and 13 year olds. The grade 7 course with the reasons left in, puzzles and ideas the course never reaches, and where the math shows up in the world. Three tracks: Sharpen (school math, and why it works), Around the corner (new ideas), Connections (where it shows up). One sheet a night, ten to fifteen minutes, and a starred problem if you want more.

1Pick a sheetStart anywhere, or follow the first thirty nights. Sharpen sheets make school math make sense. Around the corner sheets are new ideas.
2Print itEach sheet prints on three pages: two for you, and the answers on their own page at the end. Work on paper; the thinking goes better.

Sharpen the grade 7 course, with the why

Negative numbers, proportion, rates, percent error, like terms, two-step equations, inequalities, scale drawings, circumference, angles, triangles, surface area, cross-sections, chance in the long run, sampling and comparing groups.

Sharpen 1

Why minus times minus is plus

Not a rule to swallow. Follow the pattern down a times table past zero, or cancel a debt, and the sign has no choice.
Sharpen 2

Is it proportional?

Double one, the other doubles. Test a table, a graph and a story, and find the one number that ties them together.
Sharpen 3

Half a mile in a quarter of an hour

A rate is "per one". When the amounts are fractions, find the amount for one whole and the question answers itself.
Sharpen 4

How wrong is wrong?

Off by 2 cm on a pencil is bad; off by 2 cm on a house is superb. Percent error measures a mistake against the size of the thing.
Sharpen 5

Apples and oranges: collecting like terms

3x + 2x is 5x for the same reason three apples and two apples are five apples. And 3x + 2 is not 5x.
Sharpen 6

Undo it backwards

A two-step equation is a number that was multiplied, then added to. To find it, undo the last step first, like taking off shoes then socks.
Sharpen 7

At least, at most

"You must be at least 130 cm tall" is not an equation. Write it as an inequality, solve it, and draw every answer at once on a number line.
Sharpen 8

Blueprints

A scale drawing shrinks every length by the same factor. Lengths scale once, areas scale twice, and that surprises people.
Sharpen 9

Roll a wheel once

The distance a wheel rolls in one turn is a little more than three diameters, whatever its size. That "little more than three" is π.
Sharpen 10

Angle chasing

Angles on a line make 180°, angles at a point make 360°, opposite angles at a crossing are equal. Three facts unlock a whole diagram.
Sharpen 11

Can three sticks make a triangle?

3, 4 and 8 cannot. The two short sides must be able to reach each other, and that one test decides every case.
Sharpen 12

Wrapping a box

Surface area is the paper needed to cover a solid. Unfold the box into its net and it is six rectangles in three matching pairs.
Sharpen 13

Slicing solids

Cut a cube straight and you get a square. Tilt the knife and you can get a triangle, a rectangle, even a hexagon.
Sharpen 14

What "one in six" really promises

Roll a die six times and you may see no six at all. Roll six thousand and about a thousand will be. Chance is a promise about the long run.
Sharpen 15

Ask 50 people, learn about 5,000

A fair sample tells you about everyone. A lopsided one tells you only about the people you asked.
Sharpen 16

Are they really different?

Class A averages 2 marks more than class B. Whether that matters depends on how spread out the marks are.

Around the corner new ideas

The 1001 trick, square endings, letter sums, peasant multiplication, happy numbers, pentominoes, border patterns, wheels that are not round, shearing, inside and outside, party puzzles, tournaments, fair division, collecting sets, the barber, look and say, sums in a row, calendar magic, sorting, the abacus, dividing by zero, four fours, a triangle with no area and clock hands.

Corner 1

The 1001 trick

Type any three digits twice, like 482482. It always divides by 7, by 11 and by 13. One multiplication explains all three.
Corner 2

No square number ends in 7

Squares end only in 0, 1, 4, 5, 6 or 9. Ten cases prove it, and that is enough to spot a fake square at a glance.
Corner 3

SEND + MORE = MONEY

Each letter is a digit. Carrying, column by column, forces the answer, and there is only one.
Corner 4

Multiplying by halving and doubling

Halve one number, double the other, cross out the evens, add what is left. It always works, and binary is the reason.
Corner 5

Happy numbers

Square the digits and add. Keep going. Some numbers arrive at 1 and stay; the rest fall into the same loop of eight.
Corner 6

The twelve pentominoes

Five squares joined edge to edge make exactly twelve shapes. Together they cover 60 squares, and fitting them into a rectangle is harder than it looks.
Corner 7

Seven kinds of border

Slide, flip, turn, glide. Every repeating border pattern ever drawn is one of exactly seven kinds.
Corner 8

A wheel that is not round

A curved triangle rolls as smoothly as a circle and fits a square hole at every angle. Some coins are this shape, on purpose.
Corner 9

Slide the top, keep the area

Push a stack of cards sideways and its area does not change. That is why every triangle is half a rectangle.
Corner 10

Inside or outside?

A tangled loop on paper, and a dot. Count how many times a straight path to the edge crosses the loop: odd means inside.
Corner 11

The party puzzle

At any party, the number of people who shook an odd number of hands is even. Always. Count the hands two ways to see why.
Corner 12

Everyone plays everyone

Eight teams, seven rounds, nobody idle. Sit them round a table, pin one, and rotate the rest.
Corner 13

I cut, you choose

Two people can share a cake so neither envies the other. Three is harder, and the method shows what "fair" means.
Corner 14

How many boxes to collect all six?

Six toys, one per box, at random. Roll a die to simulate it, and find the answer nobody guesses: about fifteen.
Corner 15

The barber who shaves everyone

He shaves all those who do not shave themselves. Who shaves the barber? A puzzle that shook the foundations of mathematics.
Corner 16

Look and say

1, 11, 21, 1211, 111221. Read each line aloud to get the next. No digit bigger than 3 ever appears, and you can prove it.
Corner 17

Sums of numbers in a row

9 is 4 + 5 and also 2 + 3 + 4. Some numbers can never be written this way, and they are exactly the powers of 2.
Corner 18

Calendar magic

Box any 3 by 3 square of dates. The sum is nine times the middle date. Algebra with one letter shows why, and gives you more tricks.
Corner 19

How to sort a hand of cards

Swap neighbours until nothing is out of order. Count the swaps, find the worst case, and meet a faster way.
Corner 20

Calculating with beads

In practised hands a Japanese soroban adds faster than most people can type. Each rod is a digit, and carrying is something you can feel.
Corner 21

Why you cannot divide by zero

12 ÷ 0 is not infinity and not zero. Ask what multiplication would have to do, and no answer survives.
Corner 22

Four fours

Use exactly four 4s and any signs to make every number from 0 to 20. Order of operations becomes a toolbox.
Corner 23

The triangle with no area

Remove the middle of a triangle, then the middle of each piece, for ever. What is left has no area and an endless edge.
Corner 24

When do the clock hands meet?

Not at 1:05. The minute hand chases the hour hand, and the hour hand does not wait. The answer is a fraction of a minute.

Connections bikes · journeys · money · models · polls · screens · the globe · rhythm

The same math, found in the wild.

Connections 1

Why a bike has gears

48 teeth at the front and 16 at the back turn the wheel three times for each turn of the pedals. Ratios you can feel in your legs.
Connections 2

Reading a journey

A distance-time graph tells a story: steep is fast, flat is stopped, downhill is coming home.
Connections 3

Changing money

Say 1 dollar buys 0.9 euros. Convert there and back, and see who keeps the difference.
Connections 4

How big is a toy car, really?

A 1:64 model car is 64 times shorter than the real one, and more than a quarter of a million times lighter. Scale in one, two and three dimensions.
Connections 5

How can 1,000 people speak for a country?

A poll of a thousand is usually within about three points, however big the country is. Why size of sample matters and size of country does not.
Connections 6

Why films have black bars

4:3, 16:9, 21:9. Fit one rectangle inside another of a different shape and something has to give.
Connections 7

An address for every place on Earth

Two angles pin down any spot on the globe. How far north, how far round, and why a degree of longitude shrinks towards the poles.
Connections 8

Fractions you can clap

A whole note, halves, quarters, eighths, and a dot that adds half again. Rhythm is fractions that must add up to a bar.
