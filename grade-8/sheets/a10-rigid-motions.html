Slide, flip, turn, scale

Coordinate rules for translations, reflections, rotations and dilations, which ones keep lengths, and what congruent and similar really mean.

Name ____________________Date ____________

Tonight's idea

Put a shape on a coordinate grid and every move becomes a rule for the coordinates. The arrow \(\to\) means "goes to".

  • Translation (slide) by \(a\) across and \(b\) up: \((x, y) \to (x + a,\ y + b)\).
  • Reflection (flip) in the \(x\)-axis: \((x, y) \to (x, -y)\). In the \(y\)-axis: \((x, y) \to (-x, y)\). In the line \(y = x\): \((x, y) \to (y, x)\).
  • Rotation (turn) about the origin by 90° anticlockwise: \((x, y) \to (-y, x)\). By 180°: \((x, y) \to (-x, -y)\).
  • Dilation (scale) from the origin by factor \(k\): \((x, y) \to (kx, ky)\).

Why does the 90° rule work? The point \((x, y)\) is the far corner of a rectangle with sides \(x\) and \(y\) along the axes. Give the rectangle a quarter turn: the side of length \(y\) now runs along the negative \(x\)-axis and the side of length \(x\) runs up the \(y\)-axis. The corner lands at \((-y, x)\).

The first three moves are rigid motions: every length and every angle is unchanged, so the image is congruent to the original, the same shape in a new position. Dilation multiplies every length by \(k\) and keeps every angle, so the image is similar: the same shape at a different size. Its area is multiplied by \(k^2\), because width and height both scale by \(k\).

Watch one: reflect, then scale

Triangle \(P(2,1)\), \(Q(5,1)\), \(R(2,3)\). Reflect in the line \(y = x\) by swapping coordinates: \(P'(1,2)\), \(Q'(1,5)\), \(R'(3,2)\). Check a length: \(PQ = 3\) along the horizontal, and \(P'Q'\) runs from \((1,2)\) to \((1,5)\), also 3. Congruent, as a reflection should be. Now dilate by \(k = 2\): \((2,4)\), \((2,10)\), \((6,4)\). The side that was 3 is now 6, and the area goes from \(\tfrac12 \times 3 \times 2 = 3\) to \(\tfrac12 \times 6 \times 4 = 12\), four times as much.

Your turn

  1. Triangle \(A(1,2)\), \(B(4,2)\), \(C(4,6)\). Write the coordinates of the image under (a) a translation by 3 left and 1 up, (b) a reflection in the \(x\)-axis, (c) a rotation of 180° about the origin.
  2. Rotate the same triangle 90° anticlockwise about the origin. Then check that it is congruent: find \(AB\) and \(A'B'\), and find \(AC\) and \(A'C'\) using Pythagoras.
  3. Dilate the same triangle from the origin by \(k = \tfrac12\). Write the image coordinates, then find the area of the original and the area of the image. What is the ratio?
  4. Start from the point \((3, 5)\). (a) Reflect in the \(y\)-axis, then reflect the result in the \(x\)-axis. Which single rule from the list does the same job? (b) Reflect in the \(x\)-axis, then in the line \(y = x\). Compare with a 90° anticlockwise rotation. (c) Now do (b) in the other order: \(y = x\) first, then the \(x\)-axis. Is the result the same?

Stretch: turning about a different point

Rotate \((5, 3)\) by 90° anticlockwise about the point \((2, 1)\), not the origin. Method: slide everything so that \((2, 1)\) lands on the origin, rotate with the rule, slide back. Then write the rule for a general point \((x, y)\) rotated 90° anticlockwise about \((2, 1)\).

Answers

  1. (a) \((-2,3)\), \((1,3)\), \((1,7)\). (b) \((1,-2)\), \((4,-2)\), \((4,-6)\). (c) \((-1,-2)\), \((-4,-2)\), \((-4,-6)\).
  2. \(A'(-2,1)\), \(B'(-2,4)\), \(C'(-6,4)\). \(AB = 3\) and \(A'B'\) is the vertical gap from 1 to 4, also 3. \(AC = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = 5\) and \(A'C' = \sqrt{4^2 + 3^2} = 5\). Same lengths, so congruent.
  3. \((\tfrac12, 1)\), \((2, 1)\), \((2, 3)\). Original area \(\tfrac12 \times 3 \times 4 = 6\); image \(\tfrac12 \times 1.5 \times 2 = 1.5\). Ratio \(\tfrac14 = k^2\).
  4. (a) \((3,5) \to (-3,5) \to (-3,-5)\), the same as a 180° rotation. Two reflections in perpendicular lines make a half turn. (b) \((3,5) \to (3,-5) \to (-5,3)\), exactly the 90° rotation. (c) \((3,5) \to (5,3) \to (5,-3)\): a 90° turn the other way. The order matters.

Stretch. Slide by \((-2,-1)\): \((5,3) \to (3,2)\). Rotate: \((3,2) \to (-2,3)\). Slide back by \((2,1)\): \((0,4)\). In general \((x, y) \to (x-2,\ y-1) \to (1-y,\ x-2) \to (3-y,\ x-1)\). Check: \((5,3) \to (0,4)\), and the centre \((2,1) \to (2,1)\) stays put, as it must.
