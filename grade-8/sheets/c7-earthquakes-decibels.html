Earthquakes and decibels

Scales that go up by multiplying: one magnitude unit is ten times the shaking, ten decibels is ten times the sound energy. Powers of ten in the news.

Name ____________________Date ____________

Tonight's idea

Most scales add. Two metres more is two metres more, wherever you start. Earthquake magnitudes and decibels are different. Each step up means multiply.

A magnitude is read off a seismograph, which records how far the ground moves. Going up one unit of magnitude multiplies that movement, the amplitude, by 10. Two units is ×100, three is ×1000: a magnitude 7 moves the ground a thousand times further than a magnitude 4. The energy released grows faster still, by ×1000 for every two units. One unit is therefore the number that multiplies by itself to make 1000: \(\sqrt{1000} \approx 31.6\).

Sound works the same way. A decibel level compares a sound's intensity (the energy per second reaching your ear) with the quietest sound a person can hear, which is set at 0 dB. Every 10 dB multiplies the intensity by 10. So 20 dB is ×100, 60 dB is a million, and 120 dB is a trillion.

On a multiplying scale, a difference in readings is a ratio in the real thing. Subtract the readings, then count powers of ten.

Watch one: Haiti 2010 and Japan 2011

The 2010 Haiti earthquake measured 7.0. The 2011 earthquake off Japan measured 9.0. The difference is 2 units. Shaking: \(10^2 = 100\) times the ground movement. Energy: two units is ×1000, so the Japanese quake released about a thousand times the energy of the Haitian one.

Decibels run the same way. Heavy traffic at 80 dB and a whisper at 30 dB differ by 50 dB. That is five steps of ×10, so the traffic carries \(10^5 = 100{,}000\) times the intensity. Neither answer needed anything beyond a subtraction and a power of ten.

Your turn

  1. A magnitude 5 earthquake happens somewhere most days; a magnitude 8 is a catastrophe. How many times bigger is the ground movement in the magnitude 8, and how many times more energy does it release?
  2. Normal conversation is about 60 dB and a rock concert about 110 dB. (a) How many times more intense is the concert? (b) A friend says "110 is less than double 60, so it is not even twice as loud." What have they assumed, and why is it wrong?
  3. Magnitudes come with decimals. Compare a magnitude 7.5 with a 7.0. (a) By what factor is the ground movement multiplied? (b) By what factor is the energy? Hint: two half-steps make one whole step, so a half-step is the number that squares to give the whole step.
  4. (a) An earthquake releases 100 times the energy of a magnitude 6.0. Two units of magnitude multiply the energy by \(1000 = 10^3\), so each unit adds 1.5 to the power of ten. You need the number of units \(d\) with \(1.5d = 2\). Find the magnitude. (b) A headline says "magnitude 8 is twice as powerful as magnitude 4". Give the true factor for shaking and for energy.

Stretch: why doubling is 3 dB

Ten doublings is \(2^{10} = 1024\), almost exactly 1000, which is three steps of ×10, or 30 dB. So one doubling of intensity adds about 3 dB. Use this: (a) a second identical speaker next to the first adds how many dB? (b) How many dB is ×500? (c) How many 90 dB lawnmowers together make 100 dB?

Answers

  1. Three units. Ground movement \(10^3 = 1000\) times. Energy \(31.6^3\), which is \(1000 \times 31.6 \approx 31{,}600\) times, about thirty thousand magnitude-5 earthquakes at once.
  2. (a) 50 dB is five steps of ×10: \(10^5 = 100{,}000\) times the intensity. (b) They treated decibels as an adding scale and divided the readings. On a multiplying scale you subtract the readings, and each 10 of the difference is a factor of 10.
  3. (a) A whole step is ×10, so a half-step is \(\sqrt{10} \approx 3.16\): about three times the shaking. (b) A whole step in energy is ×31.6, so a half-step is \(\sqrt{31.6} \approx 5.6\).
  4. (a) \(d = 2/1.5 = 4/3\), so the magnitude is \(6 + 1.33 \approx 7.3\). Check: \(1.5 \times \tfrac43 = 2\), and \(10^2 = 100\). (b) Four units: shaking \(10^4 = 10{,}000\) times, energy \(1000 \times 1000 = 10^6\), a million times. Not twice.

Stretch. (a) Two speakers give twice the intensity: about 3 dB more, not twice the decibels. (b) \(500 = 1000 \div 2\): 30 dB minus 3 dB, about 27 dB. (c) 100 dB is 10 dB more than 90 dB, which is ten times the intensity: ten lawnmowers.
