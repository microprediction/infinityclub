Why the inequality flips

Multiply both sides by a negative and < becomes >. See why on a number line, then solve linear inequalities without ever being unsure.

Name ____________________Date ____________

Tonight's idea

\(a \lt b\) means \(a\) sits to the left of \(b\) on the number line. Every rule for inequalities asks what a move does to left and right.

Adding the same number to both sides slides both points the same way. Left stays left. Multiplying by a positive number stretches the line away from 0 (or squashes it toward 0). Left still stays left.

Multiplying by \(-1\) is different. It reflects every point through 0, to the same distance on the other side. The point that was on the right lands on the left.

Any negative multiplier is a reflection followed by a stretch, so it flips the order too. Dividing by a negative is the same flip.

Add or subtract anything: sign unchanged. Multiply or divide by a positive: unchanged. Multiply or divide by a negative: flip \(\lt\) to \(>\) (and \(\le\) to \(\ge\)).

You can also avoid the rule: if the \(x\) term is negative, move it to the other side, where it is positive, then finish. Both routes must agree, and checking one number settles any doubt.

Watch one: \(3 - 4x \le 11\)

Route one. Subtract 3: \(-4x \le 8\). Divide by \(-4\), a negative, so flip: \(x \ge -2\).

Route two, with no negatives. Add \(4x\) to both sides: \(3 \le 11 + 4x\). Subtract 11: \(-8 \le 4x\). Divide by 4: \(-2 \le x\). The same answer, read from the other side.

Check with numbers. \(x = 0\): \(3 \le 11\), true, and 0 is in the answer. \(x = -3\): \(3 + 12 = 15 \le 11\), false, and \(-3\) is not. The solution is every number from \(-2\) upwards, including \(-2\).

Your turn

  1. Solve each, and say whether you flipped: (a) \(5x - 7 \lt 13\); (b) \(-3x > 12\); (c) \(4 - x \ge 9\).
  2. Solve (a) \(2(x - 3) > 5x + 9\) and (b) \(-\dfrac{x + 1}{2} \le 4\). Check each answer with one number inside the solution and one outside.
  3. Suppose \(a \lt b\). For each statement, decide whether it must be true, and give the reason from the number line: (a) \(a + 5 \lt b + 5\); (b) \(3a \lt 3b\); (c) \(-2a \lt -2b\); (d) \(a - b \lt 0\).
  4. A student solves \(\dfrac{1}{x} \lt \dfrac{1}{2}\) by multiplying both sides by \(2x\) to get \(2 \lt x\). Test \(x = -1\) in the original. What went wrong, and what is the complete set of solutions?

Stretch: trapped on both sides

Solve \(-7 \lt 3 - 2x \le 5\) by doing each step to all three parts at once. Write the answer with \(x\) in the middle and the smaller number on the left. How many integers satisfy it?

Answers

  1. (a) \(5x \lt 20\), \(x \lt 4\); no flip. (b) Divide by \(-3\) and flip: \(x \lt -4\). (c) \(-x \ge 5\), so \(x \le -5\); flipped. (Or move \(x\) across: \(4 \ge 9 + x\), so \(x \le -5\).)
  2. (a) \(2x - 6 > 5x + 9\), so \(-15 > 3x\) and \(x \lt -5\). Check \(x = -6\): \(-18 > -21\), true; \(x = 0\): \(-6 > 9\), false. (b) Multiply by \(-2\) and flip: \(x + 1 \ge -8\), so \(x \ge -9\). Check \(x = 0\): \(-\tfrac12 \le 4\), true; \(x = -11\): \(5 \le 4\), false.
  3. (a) True: both slide 5 to the right. (b) True: stretching by 3 keeps left on the left. (c) False: multiplying by \(-2\) reflects, so \(-2a > -2b\). (d) True: subtract \(b\) from both sides of \(a \lt b\).
  4. \(x = -1\) gives \(-1 \lt \tfrac12\), true, yet \(-1\) is not bigger than 2. Multiplying by \(2x\) is multiplying by a negative whenever \(x \lt 0\), and then the sign should have flipped. Two cases. If \(x > 0\): \(2 \lt x\), so \(x > 2\). If \(x \lt 0\): the sign flips to \(2 > x\), which every negative \(x\) satisfies. Complete answer: \(x \lt 0\) or \(x > 2\). (\(x = 0\) is not allowed, since \(1/0\) is undefined.)

Stretch. Subtract 3 from all three parts: \(-10 \lt -2x \le 2\). Divide by \(-2\) and flip both signs: \(5 > x \ge -1\), that is, \(-1 \le x \lt 5\). The integers are \(-1, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4\): six of them.
