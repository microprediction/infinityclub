Everyone shakes hands

Ten people, each shakes hands with every other. How many handshakes? Count it badly, then count it well.

Name ____________________Date ____________

Tonight's idea

Five friends meet. Each one shakes hands once with every other. How many handshakes is that?

Count it badly. Each of the 5 friends shakes 4 hands, and \(5 \times 4 = 20\). But the picture has only 10 lines.

The bad count looked at every handshake from both ends. A shaking with B and B shaking with A are the same handshake. Everything was counted twice, so halve it: \(20 \div 2 = 10\).

Handshakes = people × (one fewer than the people) ÷ 2.

Count it well. Go round in order. A shakes 4 hands. B has already met A, so B has 3 new ones. C has 2, D has 1, and E has none left. \(4 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 10\).

Two honest counts of the same thing must agree. When they do, you can trust the answer.

Watch one: ten people

Each of the 10 people shakes 9 hands: \(10 \times 9 = 90\). Every handshake was counted twice, so there are \(90 \div 2 = 45\).

Check it the other way. The first person shakes 9 hands, the next has 8 new ones, and so on down to 1.

\[9 + 8 + 7 + 6 + 5 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 45\]

So handshakes give a fast way to add up a run of numbers. There is more on that in a grade 6 sheet.

Your turn

  1. (a) Draw 4 dots and join every pair. How many handshakes for 4 people? (b) Do the same for 6 people. Check both with the rule.
  2. There are 20 children in a class. Each one shakes hands with every other. How many handshakes?
  3. (a) Eight teams are in a league. Each team plays every other team once. How many games? (b) At a party everybody shook hands with everybody else. There were 21 handshakes. How many people were there?
  4. (a) Six people are in a room. Each one shakes hands with exactly 3 of the others. How many handshakes? (b) Five people each say, "I shook hands with exactly 3 others." Explain why somebody must be wrong.

Stretch: diagonals

Draw a shape with 10 corners and 10 straight sides. A diagonal joins two corners that are not next to each other. How many diagonals does the shape have? Use handshakes, not a ruler.

Answers

  1. (a) 6. Check: \(4 \times 3 \div 2 = 6\), or \(3 + 2 + 1 = 6\). (b) 15. Check: \(6 \times 5 \div 2 = 15\), or \(5 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 15\).
  2. \(20 \times 19 = 380\), and half of that is 190.
  3. (a) A game is like a handshake between two teams: \(8 \times 7 \div 2 = 28\) games. (b) Try some numbers. 6 people give 15 and 7 people give \(7 \times 6 \div 2 = 21\). There were 7 people.
  4. (a) \(6 \times 3 = 18\) counts every handshake twice, so there were 9. (b) \(5 \times 3 = 15\) would count every handshake twice. But 15 is odd, so it cannot be twice a whole number of handshakes.

Stretch. Joining every pair of the 10 corners gives \(10 \times 9 \div 2 = 45\) lines. Ten of those are the sides of the shape. The rest are diagonals: \(45 - 10 = 35\).
