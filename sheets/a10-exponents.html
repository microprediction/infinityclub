Big and tiny numbers

Exponents are a way of counting multiplications, and once you trust them they take you from the width of an atom to the nearest star without breaking a sweat.

Name ____________________Date ____________

Tonight's idea

\(2^3\) means \(2 \times 2 \times 2\). The exponent counts the twos. That one sentence gives you every rule. \(2^3 \times 2^4\) is three twos times four twos, seven twos in all: \(2^7\). So \(a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}\). Dividing cancels twos: \(2^7 \div 2^3 = 2^4\). And \((2^3)^2\) is three twos, twice: \(2^6\).

What is \(2^0\)? Use the rule: \(2^3 \div 2^3 = 2^0\), and \(8 \div 8 = 1\). So \(2^0 = 1\). What about \(2^{-1}\)? \(2^2 \div 2^3 = 2^{-1}\), and \(4 \div 8 = \tfrac12\). Negative exponents mean "divide instead".

Powers of 10 are the most useful of all, because \(10^n\) is a 1 followed by \(n\) zeros, and \(10^{-n}\) is a decimal point followed by \(n - 1\) zeros and a 1. Scientific notation writes any number as (something between 1 and 10) \(\times 10^n\): the distance to the Sun is \(1.5 \times 10^8\) km, the width of an atom is about \(1 \times 10^{-10}\) m. The exponent tells you the size at a glance, and sizes multiply by adding exponents.

Here is a number worth knowing. Light travels \(3 \times 10^5\) km every second. A year has about \(3.16 \times 10^7\) seconds. So a light-year, the distance light covers in a year, is \(3 \times 10^5 \times 3.16 \times 10^7 \approx 9.5 \times 10^{12}\) km. Nearly ten trillion kilometres, and it is the small unit of astronomy.

Watch one

How long does sunlight take to reach Earth? Distance \(1.5 \times 10^8\) km, speed \(3 \times 10^5\) km/s. Divide: \(\tfrac{1.5}{3} \times 10^{8-5} = 0.5 \times 10^3 = 500\) seconds, which is 8 minutes 20 seconds. The Sun you see is 8 minutes old.

Simplify \(\dfrac{5^6}{5^4}\): six fives over four fives leaves two fives, \(5^2 = 25\).

Your turn

  1. Simplify, giving both the power and the number: \(3^2 \times 3^3\); \(\;(2^3)^2\); \(\;\dfrac{7^5}{7^3}\); \(\;10^0\); \(\;10^{-2}\).
  2. Scientific notation: write 7 500 000 and 0.000 42 in it; write \(3.2 \times 10^5\) and \(6 \times 10^{-3}\) as ordinary numbers.
  3. The Moon is about 384 000 km away. How long does its light take to reach you? Express 384 000 in scientific notation first.
  4. Proxima Centauri, the nearest star after the Sun, is 4.2 light-years away. Write that in kilometres in scientific notation. A fast rocket travels 50 000 km/h. Roughly how many years would the trip take? (There are about 8800 hours in a year.)
  5. Estimate to the nearest power of ten: seconds in a year; heartbeats in an 80-year life at 70 per minute. Write each as \(a \times 10^n\) with one decimal place.
  6. An atom is about \(10^{-10}\) m across. How many atoms, side by side, make a line 1 mm long? (1 mm \(= 10^{-3}\) m.)

Stretch: folding paper to the Moon

A sheet of paper is 0.1 mm thick, which is \(10^{-4}\) m. Fold it in half and it is twice as thick; after \(n\) folds it is \(2^n \times 10^{-4}\) m. The Moon is \(3.84 \times 10^8\) m away. How many folds would reach it?

You need \(2^n\) to be at least \(3.84 \times 10^{12}\). A helpful shortcut: \(2^{10} = 1024 \approx 10^3\), so \(2^{40} \approx 10^{12}\). Start there and work up. (Nobody has folded a sheet of paper more than about a dozen times. Why do you think that is? Think about area as well as thickness.)

Answers

  1. \(3^5 = 243\); \(2^6 = 64\); \(7^2 = 49\); 1; \(\tfrac1{100} = 0.01\).
  2. \(7.5 \times 10^6\); \(4.2 \times 10^{-4}\); 320 000; 0.006.
  3. \(3.84 \times 10^5\) km \(\div\, 3 \times 10^5\) km/s \(= 1.28\) s. Moonlight is 1.3 seconds old.
  4. \(4.2 \times 9.5 \times 10^{12} \approx 4.0 \times 10^{13}\) km. Hours: \(4.0 \times 10^{13} \div (5 \times 10^4) = 8 \times 10^8\) hours. Years: \(8 \times 10^8 \div 8800 \approx 9 \times 10^4\), about 90 000 years. The nearest star is very far away.
  5. Seconds in a year: \(60 \times 60 \times 24 \times 365 = 31\,536\,000 \approx 3.2 \times 10^7\). Heartbeats: \(70 \times 60 \times 24 \times 365 \times 80 \approx 2.9 \times 10^9\), about three billion.
  6. \(10^{-3} \div 10^{-10} = 10^7\): ten million atoms.

Stretch. \(2^{40} \approx 1.1 \times 10^{12}\), \(2^{41} \approx 2.2 \times 10^{12}\), \(2^{42} \approx 4.4 \times 10^{12}\). So 42 folds: thickness \(4.4 \times 10^8\) m, past the Moon. The catch is area. Each fold halves the area, so after 42 folds a sheet the size of a football field would be folded to far less than the width of an atom. Thickness doubles, width halves, and halving loses that race.
