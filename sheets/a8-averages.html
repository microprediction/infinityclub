Lying with averages

A billionaire walks into a bar and everyone in it becomes a millionaire, on average. Learn which average to trust, how to measure spread, and how to design numbers that mislead.

Name ____________________Date ____________

Tonight's idea

There are three common "averages", and they answer different questions. The mean shares the total out equally: add everything, divide by how many. The median is the middle value when the numbers are lined up in order (or halfway between the two middle ones). The mode is the most common value.

Five friends earn 30, 35, 40, 45 and 50 thousand dollars. Mean 40, median 40; no disagreement. A billionaire joins them, earning 1 000 000 thousand. The mean jumps to about 166 700 thousand. The median moves from 40 to 42.5. Which number describes the typical person in the room? The median barely noticed the billionaire; the mean was dragged away by one value. That is the rule: the mean follows the total, the median follows the crowd. Newspapers that want a number to look big use the mean. House prices are reported as medians for exactly this reason.

An average alone hides how spread out the numbers are. Test scores of 50, 50, 50 and 20, 50, 80 both have mean 50. The mean absolute deviation (MAD) measures spread: find how far each number is from the mean (ignoring sign), then average those distances. The first set has MAD 0; the second has MAD \((30 + 0 + 30)/3 = 20\).

Watch one

Data: 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9. Mean \(= 40 \div 8 = 5\). Median: the 4th and 5th values are 4 and 5, so 4.5. Mode 4.

MAD: distances from 5 are 3, 1, 1, 1, 0, 0, 2, 4. Sum 12. MAD \(= 12 \div 8 = 1.5\). On average, a value sits 1.5 away from the mean.

Your turn

  1. Test scores: 72, 85, 91, 68, 85, 99. Find the mean, median and mode. Which would the student rather have on the report card?
  2. The mean of five numbers is 12. Four of them are 10, 15, 8 and 14. Find the fifth. (Work with the total.)
  3. Which average would you want for each, and why: the typical house price in a city; the shoe size a shop should stock most of; the amount of rain to expect in a month?
  4. Find the mean and MAD of 3, 7, 8, 10, 12.
  5. Class A has 20 students with mean score 70. Class B has 10 students with mean 80. Find the mean of all 30 students. Why is it not 75?
  6. Heights of 28 students were grouped: 140–149 cm: 3 students; 150–159: 8; 160–169: 11; 170–179: 5; 180–189: 1. Which group contains the median? Estimate the mean by pretending everyone in a group is at its midpoint (144.5, 154.5, …). Why is this only an estimate?

Stretch: building liars

(a) Write down seven whole numbers with mean 10 and median 4. (The total must be 70. Where does the "extra" have to go?)

(b) Alice and Bob practise free throws. Week 1: Alice scores 6 of 10, Bob scores 1 of 2. Week 2: Alice scores 1 of 1, Bob scores 9 of 10. Who had the higher percentage in week 1? In week 2? Over both weeks combined? This is called Simpson's paradox, and it fools real scientists. Explain in a sentence what made it happen.

Answers

  1. Mean \(500 \div 6 = 83.3\). In order: 68, 72, 85, 85, 91, 99; median 85. Mode 85. The student prefers the median or mode (85) to the mean (83.3); the low 68 pulls the mean down.
  2. Total must be \(5 \times 12 = 60\). The four given sum to 47, so the fifth is 13.
  3. House prices: median (a few mansions would wreck the mean). Shoe sizes: mode (you need the size most people actually buy). Rain: the mean, if you care about the total water over a year, or the median if you want a typical month; both are defensible, and that is the point.
  4. Mean \(40 \div 5 = 8\). Distances 5, 1, 0, 2, 4, total 12, MAD \(= 2.4\).
  5. Totals: \(20 \times 70 + 10 \times 80 = 1400 + 800 = 2200\), over 30 students gives 73.3. It is not 75 because class A has twice as many students, so its mean counts twice as much. This is a weighted mean.
  6. Counting up: 3, then 11, then 22. The 14th and 15th students fall in the 160–169 group, so that group holds the median. Estimated mean: \((144.5 \times 3 + 154.5 \times 8 + 164.5 \times 11 + 174.5 \times 5 + 184.5 \times 1) \div 28 = 4536 \div 28 = 162\) cm. Only an estimate because the exact heights inside each group were thrown away.

Stretch. (a) One answer: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 49. The median (4th value) is 4, so three values must be at most 4 and three at least 4; to reach a total of 70 the big ones have to be huge. (b) Week 1: Alice 60%, Bob 50%, Alice wins. Week 2: Alice 100%, Bob 90%, Alice wins. Combined: Alice 7 of 11 = 64%, Bob 10 of 12 = 83%, Bob wins. Alice took most of her shots in her bad week and Bob took most of his in his good week. Unequal group sizes let the combined figure reverse both parts.
