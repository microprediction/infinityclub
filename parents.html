For parents

Infinity Club is a set of printable math sheets for curious kids, organised by grade. Grades 5, 6, 7, 8, 10 and 12 (ages 10 to 18) are live, and other grades will follow. The aim is two things. Make school math feel easy by going one notch past it. And show the ideas just around the corner, the ones that make a child decide math is interesting.

The site was created by an applied mathematician with three school-age daughters. Everything here is open. There is no paywalled content.

How to use it

  • Start with the Start here page for your grade (grade 5, grade 6, grade 7, grade 8, grade 10, grade 12). It lists a first thirty nights in a sensible order.
  • One sheet a night, ten to fifteen minutes. Print it before dinner. Each sheet is a short idea, one worked example, four to six exercises, and a starred stretch problem.
  • Answers print on the last page. Fold it under or keep it. Checking their own work is part of the point, so let them see it once they have tried.
  • The stretch is meant to be hard. Not getting it is normal. Ten minutes of honest struggle on a stretch problem is worth more than the rest of the sheet.
  • Mix the tracks. A Sharpen sheet on a school night, an Around the corner sheet at the weekend, a Connections sheet when they are tired, works well.
  • The demos are for the screen. They are short interactive toys that go with specific sheets. A few minutes each.

What is on the sheets

In grade 6, Sharpen follows the grade 6 curriculum: ratios and rates, fractions and decimals, percentages, negative numbers, the distributive law, factors and multiples, area and volume, averages and graphs, simple equations, order of operations, exponents and units. Each sheet explains why the method works, not just how, and the exercises are a bit harder than typical homework.

Around the corner covers ideas usually met later or never: primes, binary, modular arithmetic, Pascal’s triangle, Fibonacci, Pythagoras, imaginary numbers, matrices, symmetry groups, the pigeonhole principle, parity, infinity, graph theory, probability and the Monty Hall problem, codes, logarithms, recursion, Benford’s law and more. Nothing on them needs more than grade 6 arithmetic.

Connections are sheets with more reading and less calculation, on where this math shows up: musical tuning, the scale of the solar system, magic tricks, honeycombs, binary search, map projections, sport, the calendar and voting paradoxes. A suggested first thirty nights is on the Start here page.

In grade 10 the same three tracks run at a higher level. Sharpen derives the course rather than quoting it: the quadratic formula from completing the square, the log laws from the exponent laws, circle theorems with proofs, standard deviation computed by hand. Around the corner is real mathematics that school leaves for later: proof by contradiction and induction, RSA, complex numbers as rotations, e, the first derivative and integral, Cantor, Bayes, Markov chains, chaos, entropy, groups. Connections reaches harmonics, Kepler, projectiles, mortgages and casinos, medical tests, epidemics, perspective, Elo ratings, GPS and apportionment. The sheets are built for ten to fifteen minutes; the starred problem is where any extra time goes.

Grade 8 and grade 12 follow the same pattern. Grade 8 explains the course rather than drilling it (why slope is the same everywhere on a line, converting units by multiplying by 1) and adds ideas from Euclid's algorithm to a cube in four dimensions. Grade 12 derives calculus, vectors and probability, then goes on to eigenvectors, gradient descent, the halting problem and where the mathematics shows up in rockets, clinical trials and climate.

Why the sheets are built this way

The design choices (answers on every sheet, mixed tracks, one worked example, a hard stretch, parents nearby) follow the learning-science literature. The research page lists the studies, with designs and effect sizes, and the claims that did not hold up.

Printing

Use the Print button at the top of any sheet, or your browser's print command. Sheets are laid out for Letter and A4, black and white, with ruled space for working. Turn on "background graphics" in the print dialog if the shaded example box comes out blank.

Suggestions and mistakes

If you spot an error in an answer, or want a topic covered, open an issue at github.com/microprediction/infinityclub. The whole site is plain HTML, so corrections are quick.
