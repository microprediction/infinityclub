∞ Grade 10

For bright 15 and 16 year olds. The grade 10 syllabus derived rather than memorised, the ideas waiting just past it, and where all of it shows up in the world. Three tracks: Sharpen (the school course, with reasons), Around the corner (new mathematics), Connections (where it shows up). One sheet a night, ten to fifteen minutes, and a starred problem if you want more.

1Pick a sheetStart anywhere, follow the first thirty nights, or take one of the three short routes that build a single idea over three nights. Sharpen sheets make the school course feel easy. Around the corner sheets are new mathematics.
2Print itEach sheet prints on three pages: two for you, and the answers on their own page at the end. Work on paper; the thinking goes better.
3Do the stretchEvery sheet ends with a starred problem that is harder than the rest. You are not expected to get it. You are expected to try.

Sharpen the grade 10 course, derived not memorised

Quadratics, functions, logs, polynomials, trigonometry, circle theorems, coordinate geometry, sequences, probability and statistics, each with the why and problems a notch harder than school sets.

Sharpen 1

Why the quadratic formula works

Complete the square once, slowly, and the formula falls out. Then never memorise it again.
Sharpen 2

What the roots are telling you

The discriminant counts solutions. Vieta says the roots add to −b/a and multiply to c/a, and that answers questions without solving the equation.
Sharpen 3

Functions as machines

Domain, range, composition and inverses, and why f⁻¹ is a reflection in y = x.
Sharpen 4

Shift, stretch, flip

Why f(x − 3) moves the graph right, not left, and how to read any transformed graph at sight.
Sharpen 5

A logarithm is an exponent

The log laws are the exponent laws in disguise. Solve 2ˣ = 10, and find out how many digits 2¹⁰⁰⁰ has.
Sharpen 6

Where curves meet

Lines, circles and parabolas intersecting: how many solutions, and how to find them without guessing.
Sharpen 7

Factor, remainder, and the cubes

The factor theorem, synthetic division, the sum and difference of cubes, and a polynomial detective problem.
Sharpen 8

Rational functions and asymptotes

Holes versus vertical asymptotes, what happens far away, and why 1/x is the shape of so many things.
Sharpen 9

Roots, rational exponents and rationalising

x to the power one half is a square root because the exponent laws insist. Nested radicals and a famous one that collapses.
Sharpen 10

Similar triangles and the proof habit

AA, SAS and the altitude to the hypotenuse, which proves Pythagoras in three lines.
Sharpen 11

SOHCAHTOA grows up: the unit circle

Exact values, angles past 90°, and why sin² + cos² = 1 is just Pythagoras.
Sharpen 12

Circle theorems, with proofs

The inscribed angle is half the central angle. Prove it, then use it to find a dozen angles fast.
Sharpen 13

Geometry with coordinates

Distance, midpoint, perpendicular slopes, the equation of a circle, and proving that the diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other, by algebra alone.
Sharpen 14

Sequences, series, and Gauss's trick

Arithmetic and geometric sums derived, not quoted, and the infinite geometric series that equals 1.
Sharpen 15

Conditional probability and trees

With and without replacement, independence, P(A given B), and the two-children problem, which fools most adults.
Sharpen 16

Standard deviation and the bell curve

What σ measures, 68–95–99.7, z-scores, and why correlation is not causation (with a real example).
Sharpen 17

Inequalities, absolute value, and sign charts

Absolute value as distance, quadratic inequalities by sign chart, and the AM–GM inequality.
Sharpen 18

Volumes: why the cone is a third

Cavalieri's principle, the cone, the sphere via Archimedes' argument, and scaling: double the size, eight times the volume.

Around the corner new ideas

Proof, induction, number theory, RSA, complex rotations, e, the first derivative and integral, infinite series, Cantor, matrices, conics, Bayes, Markov chains, chaos, entropy, algorithms, curved geometry, groups.

Corner 1

Proof by contradiction

Assume the opposite and watch it collapse: √2 is irrational, and the primes never run out.
Corner 2

Induction: infinitely many dominoes

Prove a statement for every whole number in two steps. Sum formulas, 2ⁿ > n², and tiling a chessboard with L-shapes.
Corner 3

The pigeonhole principle

Obvious, and yet it proves two Londoners have the same number of hairs and that some multiple of 7 is all 1s and 0s.
Corner 4

Counting without counting

Permutations, combinations and the binomial theorem: how many poker hands are a flush?
Corner 5

Fermat's little theorem

Raise a to the power p − 1 and the remainder is 1. Why, what it does to huge powers, and how it tests primes.
Corner 6

RSA: a lock anyone can close

Encrypt a message with public numbers, decrypt with a private one, and do a whole round by hand with small primes.
Corner 7

Multiplying is rotating

Complex numbers as arrows: multiply to rotate and scale, De Moivre, and the n roots of 1 sitting on a circle.
Corner 8

e to the i pi

Why e to the iθ equals cos θ + i sin θ, what it does to trig identities, and the most famous equation in mathematics.
Corner 9

The number e

Compound interest compounded forever, (1 + 1/n)ⁿ, and the function that is its own slope.
Corner 10

The slope of a curve

Secants becoming tangents: the derivative of x², x³ and 1/x from first principles.
Corner 11

Area under a curve, Archimedes' way

Slice, sum, and squeeze: the area under x² from 0 to 1 is exactly 1/3, and Archimedes knew.
Corner 12

Adding up forever

Geometric series converge, the harmonic series does not, and 1 + 1/4 + 1/9 + … equals π²/6.
Corner 13

Some infinities are bigger

Count the fractions, then prove the decimals cannot be counted. Cantor's diagonal in one page.
Corner 14

Matrices that move the plane

Rotations, shears, determinants as area, inverses, and solving two equations with one matrix.
Corner 15

Vectors and the dot product

The dot product measures alignment: angles, projections, work, and why perpendicular means zero.
Corner 16

Slicing a cone

Ellipse, parabola, hyperbola from one cone, focus and directrix, and why whispering galleries work.
Corner 17

Pythagorean triples and Fermat's margin

Euclid's recipe for every triple, rational points on a circle, and the note that took 358 years.
Corner 18

Continued fractions and the golden ratio

The best fractions for π and √2, why 355/113 is so good, and why φ is the hardest number to approximate.
Corner 19

Graphs: trees, planarity and Ramsey

Trees have n − 1 edges, K₅ cannot be drawn flat, and among any six people three are mutual friends or strangers.
Corner 20

Game theory: the prisoner's dilemma

Dominant strategies, Nash equilibrium, chicken, and why rational players can both lose.
Corner 21

Bayes' theorem

Update a belief with evidence. Base rates, the prosecutor's fallacy, and a spam filter in four lines.
Corner 22

Markov chains and how Google ranked the web

Transition matrices, steady states, and the random surfer that made a search engine.
Corner 23

Random walks and the gambler's ruin

Expected position zero, typical distance √n, and the exact probability of going broke.
Corner 24

The logistic map and chaos

x → r x (1 − x). Steady, then periodic, then chaos, and a constant that shows up everywhere.
Corner 25

Information and entropy

How many yes/no questions does a message need? Bits, Huffman codes, and why compression has a limit.
Corner 26

Algorithms and big-O

Binary search, sorting, and why some problems blow up exponentially while others stay tame.
Corner 27

Geometry on a sphere and beyond

Triangles with three right angles, the parallel postulate, and the geometry Einstein needed.
Corner 28

Groups: the mathematics of symmetry

Symmetries compose, every element has an inverse, and Lagrange's theorem says subgroups divide.
Corner 29

Area and slope undo each other

One thin strip explains the fundamental theorem of calculus. Find areas by checking slopes, then send a car home.
Corner 30

Why π knows whether a fraction cancels

Shade a 6 × 6 grid, count by inclusion–exclusion, and watch the chance of a coprime pair settle towards 6/π².
Corner 31

Lights Out: a puzzle you can prove impossible

Press a light and its neighbours flip. Clearing the board is Ax = b over bits, and a parity mask proves some boards hopeless.
