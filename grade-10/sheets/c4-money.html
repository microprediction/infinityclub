Compound interest, mortgages and why the casino wins

e in your savings account, the mortgage formula, expected value, and the house edge made visible.

Name ____________________Date ____________

Tonight's idea

Three pieces of mathematics decide more of your financial life than anything else you will learn: a geometric series, a limit, and a weighted average.

Compounding. Put \(P\) in an account paying annual rate \(r\), compounded \(n\) times a year. Each period multiplies the balance by \(1 + r/n\), so after \(t\) years \(A = P\left(1 + \frac{r}{n}\right)^{nt}\). As \(n \to \infty\) this tends to \(Pe^{rt}\), with \(e = 2.71828\ldots\) from The number e. For ordinary rates, monthly and continuous differ by a few parts in a thousand.

The mortgage formula. Borrow \(P\) at monthly rate \(r\), repay a fixed \(M\) every month for \(n\) months. Each month the balance grows by the factor \(1 + r\) and drops by \(M\). After \(n\) months it must be zero:

\[P(1+r)^n - M\left[(1+r)^{n-1} + \cdots + (1+r) + 1\right] = 0.\]

The bracket is a geometric series with sum \(\dfrac{(1+r)^n - 1}{r}\). Solve for \(M\):

\(M = P \cdot \dfrac{r(1+r)^n}{(1+r)^n - 1}\). Every mortgage, car loan and student loan in the world is this one line.

Expected value. A bet pays \(x_i\) with probability \(p_i\). Its expected value \(E = \sum p_i x_i\) is the average result per play in the long run. Casinos are built on bets with \(E\) slightly negative for you. A European roulette wheel has 37 pockets (0 to 36); an American wheel adds a 00, making 38. Same payouts, different denominator.

Watch one: a $300,000 mortgage at 6% over 30 years

Monthly rate \(r = 0.06/12 = 0.005\), \(n = 360\) months, \((1.005)^{360} = 6.0226\). So

\[M = 300{,}000 \times \frac{0.005 \times 6.0226}{6.0226 - 1} = \$1{,}798.65 \text{ per month.}\]

Total paid: \(360 \times 1798.65 = \$647{,}515\). Interest: \(\$347{,}515\), more than the house. Not a scandal, just \((1.005)^{360}\): six doublings' worth of compounding spread over thirty years.

One spin of American roulette, $1 on a single number paying 35 to 1: \(E = \frac{1}{38}(35) + \frac{37}{38}(-1) = -\frac{2}{38} = -5.26\%\). You can win tonight; you cannot win on average.

Your turn

  1. $1,000 at 5% for 10 years. Find the balance with yearly, monthly and continuous compounding (\(e^{0.5}\)). How much does "compounding forever" add over monthly?
  2. $200,000 at 4.5% annual, repaid over 15 years (\(n = 180\)). Find the monthly payment and the total interest paid.
  3. A $2 lottery ticket wins $5,000,000 with probability 1 in 10,000,000 and $5 with probability 1 in 100. Find the expected value of the ticket and the expected loss per ticket. If 50 million tickets are sold, how much does the lottery keep on average?
  4. Find the expected value of a $10 bet on red on a European wheel (18 red, 18 black, 1 green zero; even money), and of a $10 bet on a single number on an American wheel (35 to 1, 38 pockets). Which is the better bet for the player, and by how much per spin?

Stretch: a pension is a mortgage run backwards

A pot earning 4% a year must pay out $40,000 at the end of each year. How big a pot pays forever (a perpetuity)? Now show that a pot lasting exactly 25 years is \(40{,}000 \times \dfrac{1 - 1.04^{-25}}{0.04}\) by summing a geometric series, and compute it.

Answers

  1. Yearly: \(1000 \times 1.05^{10} = \$1{,}628.89\). Monthly: \(1000 \times (1 + 0.05/12)^{120} = \$1{,}647.01\). Continuous: \(1000e^{0.5} = \$1{,}648.72\). Continuous adds only $1.71 over monthly.
  2. \(r = 0.00375\), \((1.00375)^{180} = 1.9617\). \(M = \$1{,}529.99\). Total paid \(180 \times 1529.99 = \$275{,}398\), so interest \(\$75{,}398\).
  3. \(E = 5{,}000{,}000/10^7 + 5/100 = 0.50 + 0.05 = \$0.55\). Expected loss \(= 2 - 0.55 = \$1.45\) per ticket. 50 million tickets: \(\$72.5\) million kept on average.
  4. Red, European: \(10 \times \frac{18 - 19}{37} = -\$0.27\) per spin. Single number, American: \(10 \times \frac{35 - 37}{38} = -\$0.53\) per spin. Red on the European wheel loses about half as fast; it is the better of two bad bets.

Stretch. Forever: the interest alone must cover the payout, so \(0.04 \times PV = 40{,}000\) and \(PV = \$1{,}000{,}000\). For 25 years each payment due in \(k\) years is worth \(40{,}000/1.04^k\) today; summing \(k = 1\) to 25 gives \(40{,}000 \cdot \dfrac{1}{1.04} \cdot \dfrac{1 - 1.04^{-25}}{1 - 1/1.04} = 40{,}000 \cdot \dfrac{1 - 1.04^{-25}}{0.04} = \$624{,}883\). Let 25 become infinity and \(1.04^{-n} \to 0\): the million dollars again. The mortgage formula is this sum solved for the payment.
