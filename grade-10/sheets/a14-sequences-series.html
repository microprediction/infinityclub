Sequences, series, and Gauss's trick

Arithmetic and geometric sums derived, not quoted, and the infinite geometric series that equals 1.

Name ____________________Date ____________

Tonight's idea

A sequence is a list of numbers with a rule. A series is what you get when you add them up. Two kinds account for most of what you will meet, each with a sum formula you can rebuild from scratch.

Arithmetic: add the same amount each time. First term \(a\), common difference \(d\), so the \(n\)th term is \(a + (n - 1)d\). Call the last term \(l\). The sum is Gauss's trick: write the series forwards and backwards and add the columns.

\[\begin{aligned} S &= a + (a + d) + \cdots + (l - d) + l \\ S &= l + (l - d) + \cdots + (a + d) + a \\ 2S &= (a + l) + (a + l) + \cdots + (a + l) \end{aligned}\]

Every column adds to \(a + l\), and there are \(n\) columns. So

\(S_n = \dfrac{n(a + l)}{2}\): number of terms times the average of first and last.

Geometric: multiply by the same amount each time. First term \(a\), common ratio \(r\), \(n\)th term \(ar^{n-1}\). Multiply the whole sum by \(r\) and subtract:

\[\begin{aligned} S &= a + ar + ar^2 + \cdots + ar^{n-1} \\ rS &= \phantom{a + {}} ar + ar^2 + \cdots + ar^{n-1} + ar^n \\ S - rS &= a - ar^n \end{aligned}\]

Everything in the middle cancels, so

\(S_n = \dfrac{a(1 - r^n)}{1 - r}\) (for \(r \ne 1\)), and if \(|r| \lt 1\), then \(r^n \to 0\) and \(S_\infty = \dfrac{a}{1 - r}\).

The famous case: \(\tfrac12 + \tfrac14 + \tfrac18 + \cdots = \dfrac{1/2}{1 - 1/2} = 1\). Not "practically 1": the sum of an infinite series is defined as the limit of the finite sums, and that limit is exactly 1.

Watch one: \(7 + 10 + 13 + \cdots + 100\), and ten doublings

Arithmetic. \(a = 7\), \(d = 3\), last term 100. How many terms? \(7 + 3(n - 1) = 100\) gives \(n = 32\). Sum: \(\dfrac{32 \times (7 + 100)}{2} = 16 \times 107 = 1712\).

Geometric. \(3 + 6 + 12 + \cdots\) for 10 terms: \(a = 3\), \(r = 2\). \(S_{10} = \dfrac{3(1 - 2^{10})}{1 - 2} = 3 \times 1023 = 3069\). (With \(r \gt 1\) it is tidier to write \(\dfrac{a(r^n - 1)}{r - 1}\). Same thing.)

Your turn

  1. The sequence \(4, 11, 18, 25, \ldots\) is arithmetic. Find the 50th term and the sum of the first 50 terms.
  2. The sequence \(5, 15, 45, \ldots\) is geometric. Find the 8th term and the sum of the first 8 terms.
  3. A ball is dropped from 2 m. Each bounce takes it to \(\tfrac34\) of its previous height. Find the total distance it travels before coming to rest. (Hint: the first drop is 2 m; after that every height is travelled twice, down and up.)
  4. Write \(0.272727\ldots\) as a geometric series and sum it to get a fraction in lowest terms. Why does this show every repeating decimal is a fraction?

Stretch: a series with a twist

Let \(S = 1 + 2x + 3x^2 + 4x^3 + \cdots\) with \(|x| \lt 1\). Compute \(S - xS\), notice what kind of series is left, sum it, and solve for \(S\). Then find \(\dfrac12 + \dfrac24 + \dfrac38 + \dfrac4{16} + \cdots\). (Hint: factor out one \(\tfrac12\) first.)

Answers

  1. \(d = 7\). 50th term \(= 4 + 49 \times 7 = 347\). Sum \(= \dfrac{50(4 + 347)}{2} = 25 \times 351 = 8775\).
  2. \(r = 3\). 8th term \(= 5 \times 3^7 = 10{,}935\). Sum \(= \dfrac{5(3^8 - 1)}{3 - 1} = \dfrac{5 \times 6560}{2} = 16{,}400\).
  3. First drop 2 m. Then heights \(1.5, 1.125, \ldots\), each travelled twice: \(2 \times \dfrac{1.5}{1 - 3/4} = 12\) m. Total \(2 + 12 = 14\) m. Infinitely many bounces, finite distance.
  4. \(0.272727\ldots = \tfrac{27}{100} + \tfrac{27}{10000} + \cdots = \dfrac{27/100}{1 - 1/100} = \dfrac{27}{99} = \dfrac{3}{11}\). Any repeating block is a geometric series with ratio \(\tfrac{1}{10^k}\), and the formula gives a fraction.

Stretch. \(xS = x + 2x^2 + 3x^3 + \cdots\). Subtracting term by term, \(S - xS = 1 + x + x^2 + \cdots = \dfrac{1}{1 - x}\). So \(S(1 - x) = \dfrac{1}{1 - x}\) and \(S = \dfrac{1}{(1 - x)^2}\). Then \(\displaystyle\sum \frac{n}{2^n} = \frac12\left(1 + 2 \cdot \tfrac12 + 3 \cdot \tfrac14 + \cdots\right) = \frac12 \cdot \frac{1}{(1 - \frac12)^2} = 2\). If you toss a fair coin until the first head, this is the average number of tosses you need.
