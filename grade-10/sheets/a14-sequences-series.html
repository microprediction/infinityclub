Sequences, series, and Gauss's trick

Arithmetic and geometric sums derived, not quoted, and the infinite geometric series that equals 1.

Name ____________________Date ____________

Tonight's idea

A sequence is a list of numbers with a rule. A series is what you get when you add them up. Two kinds of sequence account for most of what you will meet, and each has a sum formula that you should be able to rebuild from scratch in thirty seconds.

Arithmetic: add the same amount each time. First term \(a\), common difference \(d\): \(a,\; a + d,\; a + 2d,\; \ldots\) The \(n\)th term is \(a + (n - 1)d\) (the first term has had zero \(d\)s added, the \(n\)th has had \(n - 1\)).

The sum is Gauss's trick. Write the series forwards and backwards and add the columns:

\[\begin{aligned} S &= a + (a + d) + \cdots + (l - d) + l \\ S &= l + (l - d) + \cdots + (a + d) + a \\ 2S &= (a + l) + (a + l) + \cdots + (a + l) \end{aligned}\]

Every column adds to \(a + l\), where \(l\) is the last term, and there are \(n\) columns. So

\(S_n = \dfrac{n(a + l)}{2} = \dfrac n2\bigl(2a + (n - 1)d\bigr)\): number of terms times the average of first and last.

Geometric: multiply by the same amount each time. First term \(a\), common ratio \(r\): \(a,\; ar,\; ar^2,\; \ldots\), with \(n\)th term \(ar^{n-1}\). Gauss's trick does not work (the columns are not equal), but a different trick does. Multiply the whole sum by \(r\) and subtract:

\[\begin{aligned} S &= a + ar + ar^2 + \cdots + ar^{n-1} \\ rS &= \phantom{a + {}} ar + ar^2 + \cdots + ar^{n-1} + ar^n \\ S - rS &= a - ar^n \end{aligned}\]

Everything in the middle cancels. So \(S(1 - r) = a(1 - r^n)\), and

\(S_n = \dfrac{a(1 - r^n)}{1 - r}\) (for \(r \ne 1\)).

Adding forever. If \(|r| \lt 1\), then \(r^n\) shrinks toward 0 as \(n\) grows: \((\tfrac12)^{10} \approx 0.001\), \((\tfrac12)^{20} \approx 0.000001\). So the finite sums settle down to a limit:

\(S_\infty = \dfrac{a}{1 - r}\) when \(|r| \lt 1\).

The famous case: \(\tfrac12 + \tfrac14 + \tfrac18 + \cdots = \dfrac{1/2}{1 - 1/2} = 1\). Not "approaches 1", not "practically 1": the sum of the infinite series is defined to be the limit of the finite sums, and that limit is exactly 1. Picture a square of area 1 cut into a half, a quarter, an eighth, and so on: the pieces fill the square with nothing left over.

The same idea settles an old argument. \(0.999\ldots = \tfrac{9}{10} + \tfrac{9}{100} + \tfrac{9}{1000} + \cdots\) is geometric with \(a = \tfrac{9}{10}\) and \(r = \tfrac1{10}\), so it equals \(\dfrac{9/10}{1 - 1/10} = \dfrac{9/10}{9/10} = 1\). Two names for one number. Every repeating decimal is a geometric series, which is why every repeating decimal is a fraction.

Notation. \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n} k\) means "add up \(k\) as \(k\) runs from 1 to \(n\)". So \(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{100} k = \frac{100 \times 101}{2} = 5050\), which is the sum ten-year-old Gauss is said to have produced in seconds.

Watch one: \(7 + 10 + 13 + \cdots + 100\), and ten doublings

Arithmetic. \(a = 7\), \(d = 3\), last term 100. How many terms? \(7 + 3(n - 1) = 100\) gives \(3(n - 1) = 93\), \(n = 32\). Sum: \(\dfrac{32 \times (7 + 100)}{2} = 16 \times 107 = 1712\).

Geometric. \(3 + 6 + 12 + \cdots\) for 10 terms: \(a = 3\), \(r = 2\). \(S_{10} = \dfrac{3(1 - 2^{10})}{1 - 2} = \dfrac{3(1 - 1024)}{-1} = 3 \times 1023 = 3069\). (With \(r \gt 1\) it is tidier to write the formula as \(\dfrac{a(r^n - 1)}{r - 1}\). Same thing, both signs flipped.)

Your turn

  1. The sequence \(4, 11, 18, 25, \ldots\) Find the 50th term and the sum of the first 50 terms.
  2. Find the sum of all multiples of 3 below 1000. Then find the sum of all numbers below 1000 that are multiples of 3 or 5. (Careful: some numbers are both.)
  3. The sequence \(5, 15, 45, \ldots\) Find the 8th term and the sum of the first 8 terms.
  4. A ball is dropped from 2 m. Each bounce takes it to \(\tfrac34\) of its previous height. Find the total distance it travels before coming to rest. (Hint: the first drop is 2 m; after that every height is travelled twice, down and up.)
  5. Write \(0.272727\ldots\) as a geometric series and sum it to get a fraction in lowest terms. Then do \(0.8333\ldots\) (only the 3 repeats).
  6. Offer A: 1,000 a day for 30 days. Offer B: 1 cent on day 1, doubled every day, for 30 days. Find the total of each offer. Then find the first day on which B's running total overtakes A's running total.

Stretch: a series with a twist

Let \(S = 1 + 2x + 3x^2 + 4x^3 + \cdots\) for some \(x\) with \(|x| \lt 1\). This is not geometric (the coefficients grow), but the subtraction trick still works. Compute \(S - xS\), notice what kind of series is left, sum it, and solve for \(S\).

Then use your formula to find \(\dfrac12 + \dfrac24 + \dfrac38 + \dfrac4{16} + \cdots = \displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n}{2^n}\). (Hint: factor out one \(\tfrac12\) first so it matches the shape of \(S\).) If you toss a fair coin until the first head, this sum is the average number of tosses you need. Does the answer seem reasonable?

Answers

  1. \(d = 7\). 50th term \(= 4 + 49 \times 7 = 347\). Sum \(= \dfrac{50(4 + 347)}{2} = 25 \times 351 = 8775\).
  2. Multiples of 3: \(3 + 6 + \cdots + 999\), \(n = 333\), sum \(\dfrac{333 \times 1002}{2} = 166{,}833\). Multiples of 5: \(5 + \cdots + 995\), \(n = 199\), sum \(\dfrac{199 \times 1000}{2} = 99{,}500\). Multiples of 15 were counted twice: \(15 + \cdots + 990\), \(n = 66\), sum \(\dfrac{66 \times 1005}{2} = 33{,}165\). Total \(166{,}833 + 99{,}500 - 33{,}165 = 233{,}168\).
  3. \(r = 3\). 8th term \(= 5 \times 3^7 = 10{,}935\). Sum \(= \dfrac{5(3^8 - 1)}{3 - 1} = \dfrac{5 \times 6560}{2} = 16{,}400\).
  4. First drop 2 m. Then heights \(1.5, 1.125, \ldots\), each travelled twice: \(2 \times \dfrac{1.5}{1 - 3/4} = 2 \times 6 = 12\) m. Total \(2 + 12 = 14\) m. Infinitely many bounces, finite distance (and, it turns out, finite time too).
  5. \(0.272727\ldots = \tfrac{27}{100} + \tfrac{27}{10000} + \cdots = \dfrac{27/100}{1 - 1/100} = \dfrac{27}{99} = \dfrac{3}{11}\). \(0.8333\ldots = \tfrac{8}{10} + \left(\tfrac{3}{100} + \tfrac{3}{1000} + \cdots\right) = \tfrac{8}{10} + \dfrac{3/100}{1 - 1/10} = \tfrac{8}{10} + \tfrac{3}{90} = \tfrac{24}{30} + \tfrac{1}{30} = \tfrac{25}{30} = \tfrac56\).
  6. A: \(30 \times 1000 = 30{,}000\). B: \(0.01(2^{30} - 1) = 10{,}737{,}418.23\), about 10.7 million. Running totals: after day \(n\), A has \(1000n\) and B has \(0.01(2^n - 1)\). Day 21: B \(= 20{,}971.51\) against A \(= 21{,}000\), still behind. Day 22: B \(= 41{,}943.03\) against \(22{,}000\). B overtakes on day 22, and from then on it is not close.

Stretch. \(xS = x + 2x^2 + 3x^3 + \cdots\). Subtract term by term: \(S - xS = 1 + (2 - 1)x + (3 - 2)x^2 + (4 - 3)x^3 + \cdots = 1 + x + x^2 + \cdots\), an ordinary geometric series with sum \(\dfrac{1}{1 - x}\). So \(S(1 - x) = \dfrac{1}{1 - x}\) and \(S = \dfrac{1}{(1 - x)^2}\). For the coin: \(\displaystyle\sum \frac{n}{2^n} = \frac12\left(1 + 2 \cdot \tfrac12 + 3 \cdot \tfrac14 + \cdots\right) = \frac12 \cdot \frac{1}{(1 - \frac12)^2} = \frac12 \times 4 = 2\). On average you wait two tosses for a head. Reasonable: half the time it takes one toss, and the long waits are rare but they pull the average up. Partial sums: five terms give 1.78, ten give 1.99, twenty give 2.00 to four places.
