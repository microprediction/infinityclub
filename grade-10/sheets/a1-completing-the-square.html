Why the quadratic formula works

Complete the square once, slowly, and the formula falls out. Then never memorise it again.

Name ____________________Date ____________

Tonight's idea

Start with a picture. \(x^2 + 6x\) is a square of side \(x\) with two strips of width 3 glued on. Those strips almost make a bigger square of side \(x + 3\). What is missing is the \(3 \times 3\) corner. So

\(x^2 + 6x = (x + 3)^2 - 9\). In general, \(x^2 + bx = \left(x + \tfrac{b}{2}\right)^2 - \tfrac{b^2}{4}\).

Half the coefficient of \(x\), square it, and pay it back. That one move is called completing the square, and it does three jobs at once.

Job 1: it solves every quadratic. Take \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\) with \(a \neq 0\). Divide by \(a\): \(x^2 + \tfrac{b}{a}x = -\tfrac{c}{a}\). Complete the square on the left:

\[\left(x + \frac{b}{2a}\right)^2 - \frac{b^2}{4a^2} = -\frac{c}{a} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \left(x + \frac{b}{2a}\right)^2 = \frac{b^2 - 4ac}{4a^2}.\]

Take square roots of both sides (two of them, one positive and one negative) and move the \(\tfrac{b}{2a}\) across:

\[x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}.\]

That is the quadratic formula. It is not a fact to memorise. It is what you get when you complete the square with letters instead of numbers.

Job 2: it finds the turning point. Writing \(y = ax^2 + bx + c\) as \(y = a(x - h)^2 + k\) is called vertex form. The square \((x - h)^2\) is never negative and is zero only at \(x = h\), so the graph turns at \((h, k)\): a minimum if \(a > 0\), a maximum if \(a \lt 0\). Comparing with the formula above, \(h = -\tfrac{b}{2a}\) and \(k = c - \tfrac{b^2}{4a}\).

Job 3: it tells you how many solutions there are, which is next sheet's whole story.

Watch one: \(2x^2 - 12x + 7 = 0\)

Factor the leading coefficient out of the \(x\) terms only: \(2(x^2 - 6x) + 7\). Inside the bracket, half of \(-6\) is \(-3\), and \((-3)^2 = 9\):

\[2\big((x - 3)^2 - 9\big) + 7 = 2(x - 3)^2 - 18 + 7 = 2(x - 3)^2 - 11.\]

So the graph of \(y = 2x^2 - 12x + 7\) has its vertex at \((3, -11)\), and since \(a = 2 > 0\) that is the lowest point. The equation \(2(x-3)^2 = 11\) gives \((x - 3)^2 = \tfrac{11}{2}\), so \(x = 3 \pm \sqrt{\tfrac{11}{2}} = 3 \pm \tfrac{\sqrt{22}}{2}\), about 0.65 and 5.35.

Check against the formula: \(x = \dfrac{12 \pm \sqrt{144 - 56}}{4} = \dfrac{12 \pm \sqrt{88}}{4} = 3 \pm \dfrac{\sqrt{22}}{2}\). Same answer, and now you know why.

Play with \(a\), \(b\), \(c\) and watch the vertex and roots move: infinityclub.net/demos/quadratic.html.

Your turn

  1. Write each in the form \((x + p)^2 + q\): \(x^2 + 10x + 7\) and \(x^2 - 3x + 1\).
  2. Solve \(x^2 + 8x - 20 = 0\) by completing the square. No formula allowed.
  3. Write \(3x^2 + 12x - 4\) in vertex form. Give the vertex, say whether it is a maximum or a minimum, and find the exact roots.
  4. A ball is thrown from a balcony and its height in metres after \(t\) seconds is \(h(t) = -5t^2 + 20t + 1\). Find the greatest height and when it happens. Then find, to two decimal places, when the ball hits the ground.
  5. Redo the derivation in the idea section for the case \(a = 1\): show that \(x^2 + bx + c = 0\) has solutions \(x = -\tfrac{b}{2} \pm \sqrt{\tfrac{b^2}{4} - c}\). Use it to solve \(x^2 - 7x + 12 = 0\) and check by factorising.
  6. You have 40 m of fencing and a long straight wall. You fence a rectangle using the wall as one side (so the fence covers three sides). If the two sides perpendicular to the wall have length \(x\), write the area as a quadratic in \(x\), complete the square, and find the largest possible area.

Stretch: the minimum knows everything

For \(a > 0\), vertex form says the smallest value of \(ax^2 + bx + c\) is \(k = c - \tfrac{b^2}{4a}\). Show that \(k = -\dfrac{b^2 - 4ac}{4a}\). So the sign of \(b^2 - 4ac\) tells you whether the minimum sits below, on, or above the \(x\)-axis, which is the same as asking how many times the graph crosses it.

Use that to find every value of \(k\) for which \(x^2 + kx + 9 = 0\) has exactly one solution.

Then a problem that looks like it needs calculus and does not: two numbers add to 10. What is the smallest possible value of the sum of their squares? Write the sum of squares as a quadratic in one variable, complete the square, and read off the answer.

Answers

  1. \(x^2 + 10x + 7 = (x + 5)^2 - 18\). \(x^2 - 3x + 1 = \left(x - \tfrac32\right)^2 - \tfrac54\).
  2. \((x + 4)^2 - 16 - 20 = 0\), so \((x + 4)^2 = 36\), \(x + 4 = \pm 6\), \(x = 2\) or \(x = -10\).
  3. \(3(x^2 + 4x) - 4 = 3\big((x+2)^2 - 4\big) - 4 = 3(x + 2)^2 - 16\). Vertex \((-2, -16)\), a minimum since \(a = 3 > 0\). Roots: \((x+2)^2 = \tfrac{16}{3}\), so \(x = -2 \pm \tfrac{4}{\sqrt 3} = -2 \pm \tfrac{4\sqrt3}{3}\), about 0.31 and \(-4.31\).
  4. \(h(t) = -5(t^2 - 4t) + 1 = -5\big((t-2)^2 - 4\big) + 1 = -5(t - 2)^2 + 21\). Greatest height 21 m at \(t = 2\) s. Ground: \(5(t-2)^2 = 21\), \(t = 2 + \sqrt{4.2} \approx 4.05\) s (the other root, \(t \approx -0.05\), is before the throw).
  5. \(x^2 + bx = -c\), so \(\left(x + \tfrac b2\right)^2 = \tfrac{b^2}{4} - c\), giving \(x = -\tfrac b2 \pm \sqrt{\tfrac{b^2}{4} - c}\). For \(x^2 - 7x + 12\): \(x = \tfrac72 \pm \sqrt{\tfrac{49}{4} - 12} = \tfrac72 \pm \tfrac12\), so \(x = 4\) or \(3\). Check: \((x-3)(x-4) = x^2 - 7x + 12\).
  6. The side along the wall is \(40 - 2x\), so \(A = x(40 - 2x) = -2x^2 + 40x = -2(x^2 - 20x) = -2(x - 10)^2 + 200\). Largest area \(200\) m², when \(x = 10\): a 10 m by 20 m rectangle.

Stretch. \(c - \tfrac{b^2}{4a} = \tfrac{4ac - b^2}{4a} = -\tfrac{b^2 - 4ac}{4a}\). With \(a > 0\): if \(b^2 - 4ac > 0\) the minimum is below the axis and the graph crosses it twice; if it equals 0 the vertex touches the axis, one solution; if it is negative the whole graph floats above, no real solution. Exactly one solution for \(x^2 + kx + 9\) needs \(k^2 - 36 = 0\), so \(k = 6\) or \(k = -6\). Sum of squares: if the numbers are \(x\) and \(10 - x\), then \(x^2 + (10 - x)^2 = 2x^2 - 20x + 100 = 2(x - 5)^2 + 50\). The minimum is 50, when both numbers are 5. Equal numbers always win this game; the square term is the penalty for being unequal.
