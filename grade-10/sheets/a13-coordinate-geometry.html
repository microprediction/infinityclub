Geometry with coordinates

Distance, midpoint, perpendicular slopes, the equation of a circle, and proving a shape is a rhombus by algebra.

Name ____________________Date ____________

Tonight's idea

Descartes' big idea was that a point is a pair of numbers. After that, every geometry statement becomes an algebra statement, and algebra can be checked without a ruler. Four tools do almost all the work.

Distance. From \((x_1, y_1)\) to \((x_2, y_2)\), go across \(x_2 - x_1\) and up \(y_2 - y_1\). Those are the legs of a right triangle, so the distance is the hypotenuse: \(d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}\). It is Pythagoras, nothing more.

Midpoint. Halfway across and halfway up: \(\left(\frac{x_1 + x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right)\). Average the coordinates.

Slope. \(m = \dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}\), rise over run. Parallel lines have equal slopes. Perpendicular lines have slopes that multiply to \(-1\). Why: a line of slope \(m\) goes across 1 and up \(m\). Rotate that little triangle a quarter turn and it goes across \(-m\) and up 1, so the perpendicular slope is \(\dfrac{1}{-m} = -\dfrac1m\). (Horizontal and vertical lines are the exception, since a vertical line has no slope.)

Circle. A circle is the set of points at distance \(r\) from the centre \((h, k)\). Write that with the distance formula and square both sides:

\((x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2\)

If someone hands you a circle in expanded form, like \(x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 4y - 12 = 0\), complete the square in \(x\) and in \(y\) separately to read off the centre and radius. That is the same completing-the-square from sheet a1, used for a different purpose.

The proof strategy. To prove a shape has a property: put coordinates on it, compute, conclude. "All four sides equal" means four distance calculations. "Diagonals perpendicular" means two slopes multiplying to \(-1\). "Diagonals bisect each other" means two midpoints coinciding. Choose coordinates to make the arithmetic kind: a vertex at the origin, a side along an axis.

Watch one: is there a right angle at \(B\)?

\(A(1, 2)\), \(B(4, 6)\), \(C(8, 3)\). Slope of \(AB\): \(\frac{6 - 2}{4 - 1} = \frac43\). Slope of \(BC\): \(\frac{3 - 6}{8 - 4} = -\frac34\). Product: \(\frac43 \times \left(-\frac34\right) = -1\). So \(AB \perp BC\): a right angle at \(B\).

Lengths: \(AB = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = 5\) and \(BC = \sqrt{4^2 + 3^2} = 5\). An isosceles right triangle, so its area is \(\tfrac12 \times 5 \times 5 = 12.5\), and the hypotenuse should be \(5\sqrt2\). Check: \(AC = \sqrt{7^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{50} = 5\sqrt2\). Everything agrees, which is how you know you have not made a slip.

Your turn

  1. \(P(-3, 4)\) and \(Q(5, -2)\). Find the length \(PQ\), the midpoint \(M\), the slope of \(PQ\), and the equation of the perpendicular bisector of \(PQ\) in the form \(ax + by + c = 0\).
  2. The circle \(x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 4y - 12 = 0\). Complete the square to find its centre and radius. Show that \((7, 1)\) lies on it. Then find the equation of the tangent line at \((7, 1)\). (Hint: the tangent is perpendicular to the radius.)
  3. Prove that \(A(0,0)\), \(B(5,0)\), \(C(8,4)\), \(D(3,4)\) is a rhombus: show all four sides are equal. Then show its diagonals are perpendicular, and find its area two different ways (half the product of the diagonals; base times height).
  4. Find the point on the \(x\)-axis that is the same distance from \((2, 5)\) as from \((6, -1)\). Check your answer by computing both distances.
  5. Triangle with vertices \((0,0)\), \((6,0)\), \((0,8)\). Find the centre and radius of the circle through all three vertices, and write its equation. Which circle theorem told you where to look?
  6. Where does the line \(y = 2x + 1\) meet the circle \(x^2 + y^2 = 10\)? Find both points exactly, then the length of the chord between them, in simplest surd form.

Stretch: two theorems for the price of a good coordinate choice

(a) Prove that the diagonals of any parallelogram bisect each other. Place it as \(A(0,0)\), \(B(a, 0)\), \(D(b, c)\), and work out where \(C\) must be. Then compare the midpoints of \(AC\) and \(BD\).

(b) Take any quadrilateral, \(A(0,0)\), \(B(a,0)\), \(C(b,c)\), \(D(d,e)\), and join the midpoints of its four sides in order. Prove the shape you get is always a parallelogram (Varignon's theorem). Hint: show two opposite sides of the new shape have the same "across and up".

Answers

  1. \(PQ = \sqrt{8^2 + 6^2} = 10\). \(M = (1, 1)\). Slope \(= \frac{-6}{8} = -\frac34\). Perpendicular slope \(\frac43\), through \((1,1)\): \(y - 1 = \frac43(x - 1)\), i.e. \(3y - 3 = 4x - 4\), so \(4x - 3y - 1 = 0\).
  2. \((x^2 - 6x + 9) + (y^2 + 4y + 4) = 12 + 9 + 4\), so \((x - 3)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 25\): centre \((3, -2)\), radius 5. At \((7, 1)\): \(4^2 + 3^2 = 25\). Yes. Radius slope \(\frac{1 - (-2)}{7 - 3} = \frac34\), so tangent slope \(-\frac43\): \(y - 1 = -\frac43(x - 7)\), or \(4x + 3y = 31\).
  3. \(AB = 5\), \(BC = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = 5\), \(CD = 5\), \(DA = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = 5\): a rhombus. Diagonal \(AC\) has slope \(\frac48 = \frac12\); diagonal \(BD\) has slope \(\frac{4}{3 - 5} = -2\); product \(-1\), perpendicular. Lengths \(AC = \sqrt{80}\), \(BD = \sqrt{20}\), so area \(= \tfrac12\sqrt{80}\sqrt{20} = \tfrac12\sqrt{1600} = 20\). Also base \(AB = 5\), height 4 (the \(y\)-coordinate of \(D\)): \(5 \times 4 = 20\).
  4. Call it \((x, 0)\). \((x - 2)^2 + 25 = (x - 6)^2 + 1\). Expand: \(x^2 - 4x + 29 = x^2 - 12x + 37\), so \(8x = 8\), \(x = 1\). The point is \((1, 0)\). Check: distances squared are \(1 + 25 = 26\) and \(25 + 1 = 26\).
  5. The right angle is at the origin, so the hypotenuse from \((6,0)\) to \((0,8)\) is a diameter (converse of Thales). Centre is its midpoint \((3, 4)\), radius half its length \(= \tfrac12 \times 10 = 5\). Equation \((x - 3)^2 + (y - 4)^2 = 25\). Check \((0,0)\): \(9 + 16 = 25\).
  6. Substitute: \(x^2 + (2x + 1)^2 = 10\), so \(5x^2 + 4x - 9 = 0\), which factors as \((5x + 9)(x - 1) = 0\). Points: \((1, 3)\) and \(\left(-\frac95, -\frac{13}{5}\right)\). Chord: across \(\frac{14}{5}\), up \(\frac{28}{5}\), length \(\sqrt{\frac{196}{25} + \frac{784}{25}} = \sqrt{\frac{980}{25}} = \frac{\sqrt{980}}{5} = \frac{14\sqrt5}{5} \approx 6.26\).

Stretch. (a) \(C\) is reached from \(B\) by the same move that takes \(A\) to \(D\), so \(C = (a + b, c)\). Midpoint of \(AC\): \(\left(\frac{a + b}{2}, \frac c2\right)\). Midpoint of \(BD\): \(\left(\frac{a + b}{2}, \frac c2\right)\). Same point, so each diagonal passes through the other's midpoint: they bisect each other. (b) The midpoints are \(M_1 = \left(\frac a2, 0\right)\), \(M_2 = \left(\frac{a + b}{2}, \frac c2\right)\), \(M_3 = \left(\frac{b + d}{2}, \frac{c + e}{2}\right)\), \(M_4 = \left(\frac d2, \frac e2\right)\). From \(M_1\) to \(M_2\): across \(\frac b2\), up \(\frac c2\). From \(M_4\) to \(M_3\): across \(\frac b2\), up \(\frac c2\). Same across-and-up means \(M_1M_2\) and \(M_4M_3\) are parallel and equal, which is enough for a parallelogram. (Both are parallel to the diagonal \(AC\) and half its length. The other pair is parallel to \(BD\).)
