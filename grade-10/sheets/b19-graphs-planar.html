Graphs: trees, planarity and Ramsey

Trees have n − 1 edges, K₅ cannot be drawn flat, and among any six people three are mutual friends or strangers.

Name ____________________Date ____________

Tonight's idea

A graph is dots (vertices) joined by lines (edges). Tonight, two facts about graphs, proved two different ways.

Trees. A tree is a connected graph with no cycles. Every tree with \(n\) vertices has exactly \(n - 1\) edges. Induction: one vertex, no edges. A bigger tree has a leaf, a vertex with only one edge (walk without repeating vertices; where you get stuck is a leaf). Remove the leaf and its edge: a tree with \(n - 1\) vertices, which by induction has \(n - 2\) edges. Put the leaf back: \(n - 1\).

Planarity. A graph is planar if it can be drawn with no edges crossing. Euler's formula says a connected planar drawing has \(V - E + F = 2\), where \(F\) counts regions including the outside. With \(V \ge 3\), every region is bounded by at least 3 edges and every edge borders at most 2 regions, so \(3F \le 2E\). Substitute \(F = 2 - V + E\):

\(E \le 3V - 6\) for every planar graph with \(V \ge 3\). With no triangles, regions need 4 edges and \(E \le 2V - 4\).

\(K_5\) (five vertices, all ten pairs joined) has \(10 > 9\): it cannot be drawn flat. The utility graph \(K_{3,3}\) (three houses, three utilities, nine lines) has no triangles and \(9 > 8\): impossible too. Kuratowski proved these two are the only obstacles.

Watch one: \(K_5\) minus an edge

Remove one edge from \(K_5\): \(V = 5\), \(E = 9\), and \(E \le 3V - 6 = 9\) holds. That only says "not ruled out"; to show it is planar you must draw it. Let the missing edge be 45. Draw 1, 2, 3 as a triangle, put 4 inside joined to 1, 2, 3, and 5 outside joined to 1, 2, 3. Nine edges, no crossings.

Check Euler: \(F = 2 - 5 + 9 = 6\). On the drawing: three regions inside, two outside between the spokes, and the unbounded one. Six. The inequality is a one-way test: fail it and you are non-planar; pass it and you still have to draw the thing.

Your turn

  1. A tree has 12 vertices. How many edges? A connected graph has 12 vertices and 15 edges; how many edges must you delete to leave a tree?
  2. Check Euler's formula on the cube (8 corners, 12 edges, 6 faces). The dodecahedron has 20 vertices, each of degree 3: find \(E\) from the degrees, then \(F\).
  3. In a tree every vertex has degree 1 or 3. With \(L\) leaves and \(T\) vertices of degree 3, add up the degrees (the total is \(2E = 2(n-1)\)) to prove \(L = T + 2\). Why can no such tree have 7 vertices?
  4. Use \(E \le 3V - 6\) to prove every planar graph has a vertex of degree at most 5. (Suppose every degree were \(\ge 6\) and add up the degrees.) This is the first step of every proof of the Four Colour Theorem.

Stretch: the party of six

Prove that among any six people there are three who all know each other or three who are all strangers. Hint: colour the edges of \(K_6\) red (know) or blue (strangers). Pick a person \(A\); of her 5 edges, at least 3 share a colour. Now look at the triangle those three edges point to. Why do five people not suffice?

Answers

  1. 11 edges. Delete \(15 - 11 = 4\) edges, each breaking a cycle without disconnecting the graph; which four matters, the number does not.
  2. Cube: \(8 - 12 + 6 = 2\). Dodecahedron: degree sum \(60 = 2E\), so \(E = 30\); \(F = 2 - 20 + 30 = 12\), the twelve pentagons.
  3. \(n = L + T\) and \(L + 3T = 2(n-1) = 2L + 2T - 2\), so \(T = L - 2\). Then \(n = 2T + 2\) is even, so 7 is impossible.
  4. If every degree were \(\ge 6\), then \(2E \ge 6V\), so \(E \ge 3V\). But \(E \le 3V - 6 \lt 3V\). Contradiction.

Stretch. Say \(A\) has three red edges, to \(B, C, D\). If any of \(BC, CD, BD\) is red, it makes a red triangle with \(A\); if none is, \(BCD\) is a blue triangle. Five people are not enough: colour a pentagon red and its inner pentagram blue; every triangle uses both colours. In Ramsey notation, \(R(3,3) = 6\).
