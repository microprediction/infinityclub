The cheapest can

Fix the volume, minimise the metal. Set the derivative to zero and find that the best can is as tall as it is wide. Then check it is a minimum.

Name ____________________Date ____________

Tonight's idea

A can must hold a fixed volume \(V\). Which shape uses least metal? With radius \(r\) and height \(h\), the volume is \(V = \pi r^2 h\) and the surface area is \(A = 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi r h\): two discs plus the side, a rectangle \(2\pi r\) by \(h\) when unrolled.

Two variables, one constraint. Use the constraint to eliminate one: \(h = V/(\pi r^2)\), so

\[A(r) = 2\pi r^2 + \frac{2V}{r}.\]

The first term grows with \(r\) (metal on the ends), the second shrinks (metal on the side). Between them the slope is zero:

\[A'(r) = 4\pi r - \frac{2V}{r^2} = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad r^3 = \frac{V}{2\pi}.\]

Put this back: \(h = V/(\pi r^2) = 2\pi r^3/(\pi r^2) = 2r\). The best can is exactly as tall as it is wide.

Is it a minimum? \(A''(r) = 4\pi + 4V/r^3 > 0\) for every \(r > 0\), so the curve bends upwards and the flat point is its lowest. The check is part of the answer: a zero slope could equally mark a maximum.

Write the quantity in one variable using the constraint. Set the derivative to zero. Confirm with \(A''\), and look at the ends of the allowed range.

Watch one: a 330 ml can

\(V = 330\) cm³. Then \(r^3 = 330/(2\pi) \approx 52.5\), so \(r \approx 3.74\) cm and \(h = 2r \approx 7.49\) cm. The metal used is

\[A = 2\pi(3.74)^2 + \frac{660}{3.74} \approx 264 \text{ cm}^2.\]

Cans in a shop are taller and narrower than this. Our model charges every square centimetre the same, but the ends are stamped from thicker metal than the side. Exercise 4 puts that in.

Your turn

  1. Two positive numbers multiply to 100. Write their sum as a function of one of them, minimise it, and show your answer is a minimum rather than a maximum. (Compare AM–GM.)
  2. An open-topped box has a square base of side \(x\) and must hold 4 m³. Write the area of sheet metal as a function of \(x\), minimise it, and give the dimensions of the box and the metal used.
  3. A pot has a base but no lid, and must hold a volume \(V\). Show that the least metal is used when \(h = r\), half the proportion of the closed can. Explain in one sentence why dropping the lid makes the best pot wider.
  4. Back to the closed can, but now the two ends cost twice as much per cm² as the side. Minimise the cost, not the area. Show that the cheapest can has \(h = 4r\), twice as tall as it is wide, and say which term in the cost pushed the answer that way.

Stretch: the end of the range

A wire of length \(L\) is cut in two. One piece is bent into a circle, the other into a square. Where should you cut to make the total area as small as possible? Then: where should you cut to make it as large as possible, and why does the derivative miss that answer?

Answers

  1. \(S(x) = x + 100/x\), \(S'(x) = 1 - 100/x^2 = 0\) at \(x = 10\) (we need \(x > 0\)). \(S''(x) = 200/x^3 > 0\), so a minimum. Both numbers are 10, sum 20.
  2. Height \(h = 4/x^2\), so \(A = x^2 + 4xh = x^2 + 16/x\). \(A' = 2x - 16/x^2 = 0\) gives \(x^3 = 8\), \(x = 2\) m, \(h = 1\) m. \(A'' = 2 + 32/x^3 > 0\): a minimum. Metal used \(4 + 8 = 12\) m².
  3. \(A = \pi r^2 + 2V/r\), \(A' = 2\pi r - 2V/r^2 = 0\) gives \(r^3 = V/\pi\), and \(h = V/(\pi r^2) = \pi r^3/(\pi r^2) = r\). \(A'' = 2\pi + 4V/r^3 > 0\). With one disc instead of two, widening the pot costs half as much, so the balance tips towards a wider base.
  4. Cost \(C = 2(2\pi r^2) + 2\pi r h = 4\pi r^2 + 2V/r\). \(C' = 8\pi r - 2V/r^2 = 0\) gives \(r^3 = V/(4\pi)\), so \(h = V/(\pi r^2) = 4\pi r^3/(\pi r^2) = 4r\). The doubled ends term \(4\pi r^2\) punishes radius harder, so the optimum trades radius for height.

Stretch. Give the circle \(x\) of wire, so its radius is \(x/(2\pi)\) and its area \(x^2/(4\pi)\); the square has side \((L - x)/4\) and area \((L-x)^2/16\). \(A'(x) = x/(2\pi) - (L-x)/8 = 0\) gives \(x = \pi L/(4 + \pi) \approx 0.44L\) to the circle, with \(A'' = 1/(2\pi) + 1/8 > 0\): the minimum. The maximum is at an end of the range, \(x = L\): all of the wire in the circle, area \(L^2/(4\pi) \approx 0.080L^2\), beating the all-square \(L^2/16 = 0.0625L^2\). The derivative only finds flat points inside the range. Endpoints have to be checked by hand.
