∞ Grade 12

For bright 17 and 18 year olds in their last school year. Calculus, vectors and probability derived rather than memorised, the ideas waiting at the start of university, and where all of it shows up in the world. Three tracks: Sharpen (the course, with reasons), Around the corner (new mathematics), Connections (where it shows up). One sheet a night, ten to fifteen minutes, and a starred problem if you want more.

Sharpen the final-year course, derived not memorised

Limits, the derivative rules with pictures, optimisation, curve sketching, integration by substitution and parts, volumes, differential equations, Taylor series, lines and planes, complex polynomials, the binomial and normal distributions, p-values, implicit and parametric curves.

Sharpen 1

What a limit is

sin x / x → 1, by squeezing it between two areas. Limits are about what happens nearby, never at the point, and that is why 0/0 is not an answer.
Sharpen 2

The product rule is a picture

A rectangle grows by du on one side and dv on the other. The corner piece vanishes, and (uv)′ = u′v + uv′ falls out.
Sharpen 3

The chain rule: gears

If y turns three times for each turn of u, and u twice for each turn of x, y turns six times for x. That is the chain rule, and why it multiplies.
Sharpen 4

The derivatives of sin, eˣ and ln x

Why sin′ = cos from the unit circle, why eˣ is its own slope, and why ln x has slope 1/x because it is the inverse.
Sharpen 5

The cheapest can

Fix the volume, minimise the metal. Set the derivative to zero and find that the best can is as tall as it is wide. Then check it is a minimum.
Sharpen 6

Reading a curve from its derivatives

Where f′ is zero, where f″ changes sign, and what happens far away. Sketch x³ − 3x and x e⁻ˣ without plotting a single point.
Sharpen 7

Related rates

A ladder slides, a ripple spreads, a balloon inflates. Differentiate the relationship, not the numbers, and the rates connect.
Sharpen 8

Substitution: the chain rule backwards

Spot the inner function and its derivative, change variable, and ∫ 2x cos(x²) dx becomes ∫ cos u du.
Sharpen 9

Integration by parts

The product rule read backwards. ∫ x eˣ, ∫ ln x (the trick is to let dv = dx), and the integral that comes back to itself.
Sharpen 10

Volumes by slicing

Stack thin discs and integrate. The sphere's 4/3 πr³ from circles, the cone from similar triangles, and a vase of your own design.
Sharpen 11

The equation that says "rate ∝ amount"

dy/dt = ky solved by separating variables. Radioactive decay, a cooling coffee, and why half-lives never reach zero.
Sharpen 12

Where the power series come from

Match derivatives at zero, one at a time. eˣ = 1 + x + x²/2 + …, sin x = x − x³/6 + …, and why your calculator can compute them.
Sharpen 13

Lines and planes in space

A line is a point plus a direction; a plane is a point plus a normal. Distances fall out of the dot product.
Sharpen 14

Conjugate roots and x⁴ + 4

Why non-real roots of real polynomials come in pairs, how to factor x⁴ + 4 over the reals, and the fundamental theorem of algebra stated.
Sharpen 15

The binomial distribution

n trials, probability p. Mean np, variance np(1 − p), and why variances add but standard deviations do not.
Sharpen 16

Why averages are normal

Add enough independent anythings and the sum is bell-shaped. The central limit theorem by picture, and the standard error σ/√n.
Sharpen 17

What a p-value is, and is not

The chance of data this extreme if nothing is going on. Compute one for a coin, see why 0.05 is a convention, and what a test cannot tell you.
Sharpen 18

Implicit differentiation

Differentiate both sides of x² + y² = 25 and the slope of a circle appears. Then the derivative of xˣ by taking logs first.
Sharpen 19

Partial fractions

Split 1/(x² − 1) into two simple pieces, integrate each, and get a logarithm. The same trick sums telescoping series.
Sharpen 20

Curves by parameter

x and y each as functions of t. The cycloid traced by a wheel, its slope dy/dx = (dy/dt)/(dx/dt), and the area under one arch.

Around the corner new ideas

ε–δ, e is irrational, counting primes, the Chinese remainder theorem, sums of two squares, generating functions, eigenvectors, least squares, gradient descent, a neuron, Laplace's rule, the coupon collector, Poisson, optimal stopping, Kelly, Fourier coefficients, winding numbers, the catenary, oscillators, predator and prey, fixed points, the halting problem, Hall's theorem, derangements, −1/12, inversion, knots, Lagrange multipliers.

Corner 1

What "approaches" really means

For every ε there is a δ. The definition that made calculus rigorous, used once to prove that 2x → 6, and once to show why 1/x has no limit at 0.
Corner 2

e is irrational

Suppose e = p/q, multiply the series by q!, and an integer lands strictly between 0 and 1. Fourier's one-paragraph proof.
Corner 3

How many primes are there below x?

About x / ln x. Gauss guessed it as a teenager, it took a century to prove, and you can check it against a table tonight.
Corner 4

The Chinese remainder theorem

A number that leaves remainder 2 mod 3, 3 mod 5 and 2 mod 7. Why there is exactly one answer mod 105, and how to find it.
Corner 5

Which primes are sums of two squares

5, 13, 17, 29 are; 3, 7, 11, 19 are not. The rule is the remainder mod 4, and the reason runs through Gaussian integers.
Corner 6

Counting with polynomials

Multiply (1 + x + x²)(1 + x³) and the coefficients count ways to make change. Generating functions turn counting into algebra.
Corner 7

The directions a matrix leaves alone

Most vectors get turned; eigenvectors only get stretched. Find them for a 2×2 matrix, and use them to write Fibonacci in closed form.
Corner 8

Least squares is a projection

The best-fit line is the shadow of the data on a plane. Derive the normal equations from "the error is perpendicular to the fit".
Corner 9

Gradient descent

Follow the slope downhill in small steps. How a machine finds the bottom of a bowl it cannot see, and when it overshoots.
Corner 10

A neuron is a dot product and a threshold

Weights, inputs, a sum and a switch. Train one by hand to recognise a pattern, and see what a single neuron cannot learn.
Corner 11

Will the sun rise tomorrow?

Laplace's rule: after n successes and no failures, bet (n + 1)/(n + 2). Where it comes from, by integrating over every possible probability.
Corner 12

The coupon collector

How many packets to complete a set of 50 stickers? About 50 × (1 + 1/2 + … + 1/50) ≈ 225. The harmonic numbers, with a reason.
Corner 13

The Poisson distribution

Rare events, many chances: the binomial collapses to one formula with one parameter. Horse kicks, typos, and why the mean equals the variance.
Corner 14

When to stop looking

Interview candidates one by one, no going back. Reject the first 37%, then take the next best. Why 1/e, and what it assumes.
Corner 15

How much to bet

A favourable coin, repeated. Bet too much and you go broke; bet the Kelly fraction and your wealth grows fastest. The logarithm decides.
Corner 16

Fourier coefficients by integration

Multiply a square wave by sin kx and integrate: every harmonic except the odd ones vanishes. The formula, the computation, and the overshoot that never dies.
Corner 17

Why every polynomial has a root

Walk round a huge circle and p(z) winds round the origin n times; shrink the circle to a point and it winds zero times. Somewhere in between, it crossed zero.
Corner 18

The hanging chain

A chain hangs in a cosh, not a parabola. Hyperbolic functions, why cosh² − sinh² = 1, and the equation that forces the shape.
Corner 19

y″ = −y

The equation of a spring, a pendulum and a tuning fork. Why sin and cos are its solutions, what the frequency is, and what resonance does.
Corner 20

Foxes and rabbits

Two coupled equations, no closed form, and a loop in the phase plane. Lotka–Volterra, read qualitatively.
Corner 21

Stir your coffee: a point stays still

The intermediate value theorem proves a fixed point in one dimension in two lines. Brouwer's theorem says the same in two, and why that is harder.
Corner 22

The program that cannot exist

Suppose a program could decide whether any program halts. Feed it itself, twisted, and it contradicts itself. Turing's diagonal.
Corner 23

Hall's marriage theorem

When can every job be filled by a qualified person? Exactly when every k jobs have at least k qualified people between them.
Corner 24

Derangements and 1/e

n people, n hats, nobody gets their own. The count is n!/e to the nearest whole number, and the probability settles at 0.3679.
Corner 25

1 + 2 + 3 + … = −1/12?

Not as a sum. What the famous "equation" actually says, what the zeta function is, and why the viral video's trick is not a proof.
Corner 26

Inversion in a circle

Turn the plane inside out: lines become circles through the centre, and tangent circles stay tangent, and a hard problem becomes easy.
Corner 27

Proving a knot is knotted

The trefoil cannot be undone. Colour its arcs with three colours and watch the colouring survive every move. An invariant in one evening.
Corner 28

The best point on a curve

Maximise f along a constraint: at the optimum the gradients are parallel. Lagrange multipliers with a picture, then the largest box in a sphere.

Connections space · finance · medicine · control · genetics · images · archaeology · queues · climate

The same mathematics, found in the wild.

Connections 1

Escape velocity

Integrate the inverse-square law from the surface to infinity and the energy needed is finite. 11.2 km/s for Earth, and why a black hole follows.
Connections 2

The rocket equation

Burn fuel, lose mass, gain speed. A differential equation whose answer is a logarithm, and why getting to orbit is so hard.
Connections 3

What an option is worth

One step up or down, no free lunch. Price a stock option by building a portfolio that copies it, and see why the real probability does not appear.
Connections 4

How many patients does a trial need?

Randomisation, the size of the effect, the noise, and the power. The sample-size formula from the standard error, with real numbers.
Connections 5

Cruise control

Measure the error, push back in proportion, and the car settles. Feedback as a differential equation, and why too much gain makes it wobble.
Connections 6

Why recessive genes do not die out

Allele frequencies p and q give genotypes p², 2pq, q². One generation of random mating and the mix is stable. Then a dose of selection.
Connections 7

How a photo gets ten times smaller

Blocks of pixels, the cosine transform, and throwing away the high frequencies nobody sees. Fourier in your phone.
Connections 8

Radiocarbon dating

Carbon-14 halves every 5,730 years. From the ratio left in a sample to its age, with the decay equation, and what makes the answer uncertain.
Connections 9

Why the queue is longer than you think

Arrivals at random, one server. Utilisation ρ, average queue ρ/(1 − ρ), and why a server at 90% has a queue nine times longer than at 50%.
Connections 10

Why the Earth is not frozen

Sunlight in, heat radiated out as T⁴. Balance them and the planet should be −18 °C. The greenhouse effect is the 33 degrees of difference.
