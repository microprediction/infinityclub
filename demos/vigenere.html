The Vigenère cipher

A Caesar cipher shifts every letter by the same amount. Vigenère uses a keyword: the first letter of the message is shifted by the first letter of the key (A = 0, B = 1, …), the second by the second, and the key repeats. The same plaintext letter comes out differently each time, which ruins simple letter counting. For 300 years it was called "the unbreakable cipher".

What's going on

With one shift, the tallest bar in the ciphertext is almost always E in disguise, and the whole thing falls in minutes. With a keyword of length 5, each letter of the message is shifted by one of five different amounts, so the counts are smeared out and the bars go flat. There is no single tallest bar to attack.

In 1863 Friedrich Kasiski noticed the weakness. Common words like THE land on the same part of the key whenever their distance apart is a multiple of the key length, and then they encrypt identically. Find repeated chunks in the ciphertext, measure the distances between them, and the key length is a factor of all those distances. Once you know the length is 5, the cipher is just five Caesar ciphers interleaved, and each one breaks the old way.
