What's going on

Rabbits R breed at rate a and are eaten at a rate proportional to how often a rabbit meets a fox, bRF. Foxes F die at rate c and breed in proportion to the rabbits they catch, dRF. That gives the Lotka–Volterra equations dR/dt = aR − bRF and dF/dt = −cF + dRF. There is no formula for R(t) and F(t); the demo steps the equations forward numerically (a fourth-order Runge–Kutta scheme, step 0.005) and draws what comes out.

Both derivatives vanish at the equilibrium R* = c/d, F* = a/b, the grey cross. Everywhere else the populations move: lots of rabbits feed a fox boom, the boom eats the rabbits down, the foxes then starve, and the rabbits recover. In the phase plane that is a loop running anticlockwise, and the fox peak lags the rabbit peak by about a quarter of a cycle.

Why a closed loop rather than a spiral? Divide the two equations and separate variables, and you find that V = dR − c ln R + bF − a ln F never changes along a solution. Each loop is a level curve of V, like a contour line on a map. The readout checks how well the numerics keep V constant. Two more facts to test: near the cross the period is close to 2π/√(ac), and the time-average of R over a cycle is exactly c/d, the same for every loop. Cull foxes (raise c) and the average rabbit population does not change at all; the average fox population a/b does not depend on c either, but the loops swing wider.
