Growth race

Three runners. One adds the same amount every step (a × n). One is the square (n²). One multiplies by the same factor every step (bn). Give the linear runner a huge head start and the exponential a tiny factor. See who wins anyway.

What's going on

Adding grows in a straight line. Squaring bends upward, but each step adds only a bit more than the last. Multiplying is different in kind: each step adds a fixed fraction of what you already have, so the additions themselves grow. Eventually that beats any polynomial, no matter how small the factor b or how big the head start a.

Switch on the log scale. The exponential becomes a straight line, because a log turns multiplication into addition. The square becomes a curve that flattens. Straight on a log chart is the signature of exponential growth, which is how scientists spot it in epidemics, bank balances and bacteria.

Try b = 1.1, which is only 10% growth per step, and a = 50. Push N to 40 and watch where the lines cross. Then imagine N = 100.
