Quadratic explorer

Slide a, b and c, or grab the vertex and drag it around. The three formulas under the graph (standard, vertex and factored form) are the same parabola written three ways. Watch which numbers change and which stay put, and watch the two roots meet and vanish as the discriminant crosses zero.

What's going on

Standard form y = ax² + bx + c tells you c at a glance (the y-intercept) and not much else. Vertex form y = a(x − h)² + k tells you the turning point (h, k) and the axis of symmetry x = h. Completing the square converts one into the other, and the conversion gives h = −b/(2a) and k = c − b²/(4a). Factored form y = a(x − r₁)(x − r₂) tells you the roots, when there are real ones.

The discriminant Δ = b² − 4ac decides how many real roots there are. Geometrically, Δ/(4a²) is how far the vertex sits below the axis in units that make the parabola look like x², so Δ > 0 means the vertex is on the far side of the x-axis from the arms (two roots), Δ = 0 means the vertex touches the axis (one repeated root), Δ < 0 means the parabola never reaches the axis.

Drag the vertex straight up and down: a stays fixed, h stays fixed, so b stays fixed, and only c moves. Drag it sideways: now b and c both change but a does not, because the shape of a parabola is set by a alone. Every parabola with the same a is the same shape, just moved.
