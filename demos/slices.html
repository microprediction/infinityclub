What's going on

The flat face you see when you cut straight through a solid is called a cross-section. Cut a cube level and the cross-section is a square. Tilt the knife and the shape changes.

Every side of the slice is where the knife crosses one face of the cube. A flat knife crosses a flat face in one straight line, so each face gives at most one side. A cube has six faces. That is why you can get 3, 4, 5 or 6 sides, and why no slice of a cube has 7 sides.

A triangle comes from slicing off a corner: the knife crosses the three faces that meet there. For a hexagon the knife must cross all six faces at once. Tilt it so it leans the same way to all three directions and push it through the middle of the cube. Get the tilt exactly right and all six sides are equal.
