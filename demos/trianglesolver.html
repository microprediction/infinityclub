What's going on

Two rules solve every triangle. The cosine rule, a² = b² + c² − 2bc cos A, is Pythagoras with a correction term for an angle that is not 90°. Use it when you have three sides, or two sides and the angle between them. The sine rule, a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C, says each side is in the same ratio to the sine of the angle facing it. Use it when you know a side and the angle opposite it.

When two angles are known the third is free, because the three add to 180°. That is why ASA and AAS are easy: find the third angle first, then use the sine rule twice.

SSA is the awkward one. The sine rule gives sin B, but two angles between 0° and 180° share each sine: B and 180° − B. Picture side a as a rod hinged at C, swinging down towards the base. If it is too short it never reaches (no triangle). If it is at least as long as b it reaches once. In between (when A is less than 90°) it reaches the base in two places, and both triangles are real. Try a = 7, b = 10, A = 40°, then make a smaller until the two triangles merge and disappear.
