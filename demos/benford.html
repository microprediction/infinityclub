Benford's law

Take a long list of numbers and look only at the first digit of each. You would expect 1 through 9 to show up equally, about 11% each. For most real-world lists they do not. Pick a source and count.

What's going on

Benford's law says that in many lists the first digit is 1 about 30.1% of the time, 2 about 17.6%, down to 9 at 4.6%. The formula is log10(1 + 1/d). The orange outlines show that prediction; the blue bars are what you counted.

Why? Think about something that grows by multiplying, like a number that keeps doubling. To get from 1,000 to 2,000 it has to double once, but to get from 9,000 to 10,000 it needs only a small nudge. So the number spends a long time with a leading 1 and hurries through the leading 9s. Powers of 2, Fibonacci numbers and factorials all grow by multiplying, so they all obey the law almost perfectly.

Squares grow too slowly and random numbers from 1 to 1000 do not grow at all, so they break the pattern. Tax inspectors use Benford's law to catch invented figures: people making up numbers spread the first digits evenly, and real accounts do not.
