Times tables on a circle

Put n points around a circle, numbered 0 to n−1 like a giant clock. Draw a line from each point k to the point k × m, wrapping around the clock. That is all. Then press Animate.

What's going on

"Wrapping around the clock" is clock arithmetic: the line from k goes to (k × m) mod n. With m = 2 the lines wrap into a heart shape called a cardioid, the same curve you see when light reflects inside a coffee cup. With m = 3 you get two lobes, with m = 4 three lobes, and so on.

Multiplication by m = n − 1 is the same as multiplication by −1: it sends k to −k on the clock. With n = 100 and m = 99, each endpoint is reflected across the vertical diameter, so the lines go horizontally across the circle. Try m = 51 with n = 100: even-numbered points stay put, while odd-numbered points connect to the point directly opposite them, making 25 diameters.

The slider allows fractional m so the animation flows, but the real pattern lives at the whole numbers.
