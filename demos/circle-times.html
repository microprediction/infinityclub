Times tables on a circle

Put n points around a circle, numbered 0 to n−1 like a giant clock. Draw a line from each point k to the point k × m, wrapping around the clock. With n = 10 and m = 2: 1 goes to 2, 3 goes to 6, and 7 goes to 14, which is 4 on a 10-hour clock. Drag n down to 10 to see those lines, then back up. Then press Animate.

What's going on

"Wrapping around the clock" is clock arithmetic: the line from k goes to (k × m) mod n. With m = 2 the lines wrap into a heart shape called a cardioid, the same curve you see when light reflects inside a coffee cup. With m = 3 you get two lobes, with m = 4 three lobes, and so on.

Multiplication by m = n − 1 is the same as multiplication by −1 (one step back on the clock), so m = 99 with n = 100 gives a mirror image of m = 1: every line just goes across. Try m = 51 with n = 100 and think about why it looks like m = 2 reflected.

The slider allows fractional m so the animation flows, but the real pattern lives at the whole numbers.
