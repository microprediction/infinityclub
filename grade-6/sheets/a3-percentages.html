Percentages in your head

Two building blocks, one swap trick nobody teaches, and the reason a 20% rise followed by a 20% fall leaves you poorer.

Name ____________________Date ____________

Tonight's idea

"Percent" means "per hundred", so a percentage is just a fraction with 100 on the bottom. Two building blocks do nearly all the work: 10% (move the decimal point one place) and 1% (move it two). 15% of 80 is 10% plus 5%: \(8 + 4 = 12\). 23% of 300 is \(2 \times 30 + 3 \times 3 = 69\).

Here is a trick that feels like cheating. \(a\%\) of \(b\) equals \(b\%\) of \(a\). So 8% of 25 is the same as 25% of 8, which is 2. Why? Both equal \(\tfrac{a \times b}{100}\). Multiplication does not care about order, so you may swap whenever one side is easier.

The last idea is the one adults get wrong. Percentages are always of something, and that something changes. Go up 25% from 80 and you reach 100. Go down 25% from 100 and you reach 75, not 80. The 25% on the way down was 25% of a bigger number. Any rise of \(x\%\) followed by a fall of \(x\%\) leaves you below where you started.

So treat a percentage change as a multiplier. Up 25% means multiply by 1.25. Down 25% means multiply by 0.75. Chains of changes become chains of multiplications, and reversing a change means dividing by its multiplier.

Watch one

A jacket is 30% off, and at the till there is an extra 10% off the sale price. What is the total discount?

Multipliers: \(0.7 \times 0.9 = 0.63\). You pay 63%, so the total discount is 37%, not 40%. The second 10% was taken off an already-reduced price.

Reverse: after a 20% discount a game costs $48. Original price? The discount multiplier is 0.8, so divide: \(48 \div 0.8 = 60\). Check: 20% of 60 is 12, and \(60 - 12 = 48\).

Your turn

  1. In your head, using 10% and 1% blocks: 15% of 60, 35% of 80, 2.5% of 400, 12% of 50.
  2. Use the swap trick: 4% of 75, 16% of 25, 18% of 50, 36% of 25.
  3. A price goes up 25% and then down 25%. Start at $80. Where do you end up? Now start at $200. What percentage of the starting price is lost each time?
  4. A phone costs $540 after a 10% price rise. What did it cost before? (Dividing by 1.1 is the move. Do not subtract 10%.)
  5. A town's population of 200 grows by 50%, then shrinks by 50%. What is it now? Write the chain as a single multiplier.
  6. Find the single percentage change equivalent to "up 10%, up 10%, up 10%". Is it more or less than 30%? Why?

Stretch: the rule of 70

Money growing at 7% a year does not take \(100 \div 7 \approx 14\) years to double. It takes about 10, because each year's 7% is taken on a bigger pile. The handy rule: doubling time ≈ 70 ÷ (percentage rate). At 7% that gives 10 years; at 10% about 7 years; at 5% about 14 years.

Check the rule for 10% by multiplying by 1.1 seven times (a calculator is allowed). Then: if $100 grows at 7% a year for 30 years, roughly how many doublings is that, and roughly how much money? Why is a loan at 20% so dangerous?

Answers

  1. 9 (6 + 3), 28 (24 + 4), 10 (8 + 2), 6 (5 + 1).
  2. 75% of 4 = 3. 25% of 16 = 4. 50% of 18 = 9. 25% of 36 = 9.
  3. $80 → $100 → $75. $200 → $250 → $187.50. Each time you lose \(\tfrac{1}{16}\), which is 6.25%, because \(1.25 \times 0.75 = 0.9375\).
  4. \(540 \div 1.1 = 490.909\ldots\), so about $490.91. Subtracting 10% of 540 would wrongly give $486.
  5. \(200 \times 1.5 \times 0.5 = 200 \times 0.75 = 150\).
  6. \(1.1^3 = 1.331\), a 33.1% rise. More than 30%, because the second and third 10% are taken on bigger amounts.

Stretch. \(1.1^7 \approx 1.95\), so seven years very nearly doubles the money. Thirty years at 7% is about three doublings: \(100 \to 200 \to 400 \to 800\). (Exactly, \(1.07^{30} \approx 7.6\), so about $760.) At 20% the doubling time is about 3.5 years, so a debt left alone for ten years grows roughly \(2^3 = 8\) times. (Exact: \(1.2^{10} \approx 6.2\); the rule is an approximation that gets rougher for big rates.)
