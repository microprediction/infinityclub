Will food keep up with people?

In 1798 Malthus argued that population multiplies while food only adds. Compare an exponential with a line, find where they cross, and see what he missed.

Name ____________________Date ____________

Tonight's idea

In 1798 the English writer Thomas Malthus published an essay on population. His argument, roughly: a population with nothing holding it back multiplies. If it doubles in 25 years, it doubles again in the next 25. Food, he thought, could at best add: each generation might farm enough to feed a fixed number more.

So population is an exponential and food is a line. A quantity that multiplies always overtakes one that adds, however big a head start the line is given. Malthus concluded that hunger, disease or fewer births would have to hold numbers down.

What he missed. The arithmetic was sound, and in his day harvests did grow slowly. The assumption about food did not last. In the two centuries since, better crops, fertiliser and machines made harvests multiply as well, and food production grew faster than population. Birth rates also fell in many countries. A model is only as good as its assumptions.

Watch one: a land of 1 million in 1800

People double every 25 years. Food feeds 4 million, and each 25 years farmers can feed 3 million more. After \(n\) periods of 25 years, in millions: people \(= 2^n\), food feeds \(4 + 3n\).

Year180018251850187519001925
People \(2^n\)12481632
Food feeds \(4 + 3n\)4710131619

They cross at \(n = 4\), the year 1900: \(2^4 = 16 = 4 + 3(4)\). By 1925 there are 32 million people and food for 19 million.

Your turn

  1. Another land has 2 million people in 1800, doubling every 25 years. Food feeds 10 million, plus 2 million more each 25 years. (a) Write a formula for each after \(n\) periods. (b) Make a table. In which year do they cross?
  2. Give food a huge head start: it feeds 100 million, plus 10 million more each 25 years. The people start at 1 million and double every 25 years. Between which two years do they overtake the food?
  3. A population grows by 3% a year, so each year it is multiplied by 1.03. (a) Show that after 25 years it has roughly doubled. (b) What does 1% a year multiply it by in 25 years?
  4. Suppose food multiplies too. People start at 1 million and double each period. Food feeds 4 million and is multiplied by 1.5 each period. (a) Do they still cross? Between which periods? (b) What if food also doubles each period? (c) What decides whether food keeps up?

Stretch: what really happened

The world held about 1 billion people around 1800 and about 8 billion around 2025. (a) If it had doubled every 25 years, how many would there be in 2025? (b) How many doublings really happened, and what was the average doubling time?

Answers

  1. (a) People \(= 2 \times 2^n\), food feeds \(10 + 2n\). (b) People: 2, 4, 8, 16, 32. Food: 10, 12, 14, 16, 18. They cross at \(n = 3\), the year 1875, at 16 million.
  2. Compare \(2^n\) with \(100 + 10n\). At \(n = 7\): 128 against 170. At \(n = 8\): 256 against 180. The people overtake between 1975 and 2000. A head start of a hundred times bought less than two centuries.
  3. (a) \(1.03^{25} \approx 2.09\), a little more than double. (b) \(1.01^{25} \approx 1.28\), a rise of 28%. A small change in the yearly rate makes a large change over 25 years.
  4. (a) Yes. At \(n = 4\): 16 against \(4 \times 1.5^4 = 20.25\). At \(n = 5\): 32 against about 30.4. They cross between periods 4 and 5. (b) Then food feeds \(4 \times 2^n\), always exactly 4 times the population. They never cross. (c) The multipliers. If food's multiplier is at least as big as the population's, food keeps its lead. If it is smaller, any head start is used up in the end.

Stretch. (a) From 1800 to 2025 is 225 years, which is 9 periods of 25. \(2^9 = 512\) billion. (b) \(8 = 2^3\), so three doublings in 225 years: one every 75 years on average. The population did grow by multiplying, but far more slowly than a doubling every 25 years.
