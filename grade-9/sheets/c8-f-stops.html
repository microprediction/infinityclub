Why camera settings go 2, 2.8, 4, 5.6

Each step halves the light. The numbers are powers of √2, because the light depends on the area of the opening.

Name ____________________Date ____________

Tonight's idea

A camera lens has a round opening that can be made wider or narrower, called the aperture. Its size is given as an f-number \(N\): the focal length \(f\) of the lens (a fixed length, such as 50 mm) divided by the diameter \(d\) of the opening. So \(d = f/N\), and the setting is written f/4. A bigger f-number means a smaller hole.

The light that gets through depends on the area of the opening, and area goes with the diameter squared.

Light let in is proportional to \(d^2\), so to \(\dfrac{1}{N^2}\).

Photographers want each step, called a stop, to halve the light. Halving the diameter would cut the area to a quarter. To halve the area, divide the diameter by \(\sqrt2\), which means multiplying \(N\) by \(\sqrt2\).

Start at 1 and keep multiplying by \(\sqrt2\): 1.41, 2, 2.83, 4, 5.66, 8, 11.3, 16. Rounded, that is the scale on a lens: 1.4, 2, 2.8, 4, 5.6, 8, 11, 16. Every second number doubles, because \(\sqrt2 \times \sqrt2 = 2\).

Watch one: a 50 mm lens

At f/2 the opening is \(50 \div 2 = 25\) mm across. Its area is \(\pi \times 12.5^2 \approx 491\) mm².

One stop on is f/2.8, which is really \(2\sqrt2\). The diameter is \(25 \div \sqrt2 \approx 17.7\) mm and the area is \(\pi \times 8.84^2 \approx 245\) mm²: half.

At f/4 the diameter is 12.5 mm and the area about 123 mm²: a quarter. Check with the rule: \(N\) doubled from 2 to 4, and \(1/2^2 = \tfrac14\).

Your turn

  1. (a) Work out \((\sqrt2)^n\) for \(n = 1\) to 8, to 2 decimal places. Which number on the lens scale is not what correct rounding would give? (b) Find the next two stops after f/16.
  2. A lens has focal length 100 mm. (a) How wide is the opening at f/4 and at f/8? (b) How many times as much light does f/4 let in? How many stops apart are they?
  3. (a) How many times as much light does f/2 let in as f/16? How many stops is that? (b) The total light in a photo is the light let in each second times the time the shutter stays open. A photo is right at f/4 for \(\tfrac{1}{500}\) s. What time gives the same total at f/8?
  4. A 50 mm lens and a 200 mm lens are both set to f/4. (a) Find each opening's diameter. (b) How many times the area is the larger opening? (c) The 200 mm lens makes everything in the picture 4 times as wide and 4 times as tall. Why are the two pictures equally bright?

Stretch: thirds of a stop

Many cameras click in thirds of a stop: three equal multiplying steps take \(N\) from one full stop to the next. What does each click multiply \(N\) by? Find the two settings between f/4 and f/5.6.

Answers

  1. (a) 1.41, 2, 2.83, 4, 5.66, 8, 11.31, 16. To two figures 5.66 rounds to 5.7, but lenses are marked 5.6. The others round as marked. (b) \(16\sqrt2 \approx 22.6\), marked f/22, then f/32.
  2. (a) \(100/4 = 25\) mm and \(100/8 = 12.5\) mm. (b) The diameter is twice as big, so the area and the light are \(2^2 = 4\) times as much. Two stops, since each stop is a factor of 2.
  3. (a) \(N\) is 8 times bigger, so the light ratio is \(8^2 = 64 = 2^6\): six stops. Count them: 2, 2.8, 4, 5.6, 8, 11, 16. (b) f/8 lets in a quarter of the light each second, so the shutter must stay open 4 times as long: \(\tfrac{4}{500} = \tfrac{1}{125}\) s.
  4. (a) \(50/4 = 12.5\) mm and \(200/4 = 50\) mm. (b) The diameter is 4 times as big, so the area is 16 times as big. (c) The long lens collects 16 times the light but spreads it over \(4 \times 4 = 16\) times the area. The light on each bit of the picture is the same. That is why photographers quote \(N\) and not the diameter: equal f-numbers give equal brightness on any lens.

Stretch. Call the multiplier \(m\). Three clicks make one stop, so \(m^3 = \sqrt2 \approx 1.414\), and the cube root gives \(m \approx 1.122\). (Since \(m^6 = 2\), it is the sixth root of 2.) From 4: \(4 \times 1.122 \approx 4.49\) and \(4.49 \times 1.122 \approx 5.04\), then 5.66. Cameras show f/4.5 and f/5.0.
