A slice of a circle

An arc is a fraction of the circumference and a sector the same fraction of the area. The fraction is the angle out of 360.

Name ____________________Date ____________

Tonight's idea

Cut a circle from its centre, like a pizza. The slice is a sector. Its curved edge is an arc. The angle at the centre, \(\theta\) (theta), says how big the slice is.

A full turn is 360°. Turning a circle about its centre changes nothing, so every 1° slice is identical to every other, and 360 of them make the whole circle. Each has \(\tfrac{1}{360}\) of the circumference and \(\tfrac{1}{360}\) of the area. A slice of \(\theta\) degrees is \(\theta\) of these, so it has the fraction \(\tfrac{\theta}{360}\) of both.

Arc length \(= \dfrac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r\)    Sector area \(= \dfrac{\theta}{360} \times \pi r^2\)

There is nothing new to memorise. Find the fraction first, then take that fraction of the circumference \(2\pi r\) or the area \(\pi r^2\). A 90° sector is a quarter of the circle; a 60° sector is a sixth. Leave \(\pi\) in the answer for an exact value, and turn it into a decimal only at the end.

Watch one: a 120° sector of radius 6 cm

Fraction: \(\tfrac{120}{360} = \tfrac13\).

Arc: \(\tfrac13 \times 2\pi \times 6 = 4\pi \approx 12.57\) cm.

Area: \(\tfrac13 \times \pi \times 6^2 = 12\pi \approx 37.70\) cm².

The perimeter of the slice is the arc plus two straight edges: \(4\pi + 12 \approx 24.57\) cm. Forgetting the two radii is the usual slip.

Where you met the whole circle: Roll a wheel once (grade 7) for \(2\pi r\), and Why a pizza is πr² (grade 6).

Your turn

  1. Find the arc length and the area of each sector, exactly and to two decimal places. (a) Radius 10 cm, angle 90°. (b) Radius 5 cm, angle 36°.
  2. The minute hand of a clock is 12 cm long. In 25 minutes, (a) what angle does it turn through, (b) how far does its tip travel, and (c) what area does it sweep over?
  3. Work backwards. (a) An arc of a circle of radius 12 cm is \(5\pi\) cm long. What is the angle at the centre? (b) A 60° sector has area \(6\pi\) cm². What is its radius?
  4. One slice is 40° of a pizza of radius 18 cm. Another is 90° of a pizza of radius 12 cm. (a) Which slice has more pizza? (b) Which has more crust along its curved edge? (c) Explain how both answers can be true.

Stretch: a sector as a triangle

(a) Show that sector area \(= \tfrac12 \times \text{arc length} \times r\), whatever the angle. Which triangle formula does that look like? (b) Find the angle of a sector whose arc is exactly as long as its radius.

Answers

  1. (a) A quarter: arc \(\tfrac14 \times 20\pi = 5\pi \approx 15.71\) cm; area \(\tfrac14 \times 100\pi = 25\pi \approx 78.54\) cm². (b) A tenth: arc \(\tfrac{1}{10} \times 10\pi = \pi \approx 3.14\) cm; area \(\tfrac{1}{10} \times 25\pi = 2.5\pi \approx 7.85\) cm².
  2. (a) 60 minutes is 360°, so each minute is 6° and 25 minutes is 150°. (b) The fraction is \(\tfrac{150}{360} = \tfrac{5}{12}\): \(\tfrac{5}{12} \times 24\pi = 10\pi \approx 31.4\) cm. (c) \(\tfrac{5}{12} \times 144\pi = 60\pi \approx 188.5\) cm².
  3. (a) The circumference is \(24\pi\), so the fraction is \(\tfrac{5\pi}{24\pi} = \tfrac{5}{24}\) and the angle is \(\tfrac{5}{24} \times 360 = 75^\circ\). (b) A sixth of the circle is \(6\pi\), so the circle is \(36\pi = \pi r^2\) and \(r = 6\) cm.
  4. (a) Neither: \(\tfrac19 \times 324\pi = 36\pi\) and \(\tfrac14 \times 144\pi = 36\pi\), about 113.1 cm² each. (b) The second: its arc is \(\tfrac14 \times 24\pi = 6\pi \approx 18.8\) cm against \(\tfrac19 \times 36\pi = 4\pi \approx 12.6\) cm. (c) Area depends on \(r^2\) but arc only on \(r\). The first slice is long and thin, the second short and wide.

Stretch. (a) \(\tfrac12 \times \left(\tfrac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r\right) \times r = \tfrac{\theta}{360} \times \pi r^2\), the sector area. It looks like \(\tfrac12 \times \text{base} \times \text{height}\), with the arc as base and the radius as height. Check with question 4: \(\tfrac12 \times 4\pi \times 18 = 36\pi\). (b) \(\tfrac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r = r\) gives \(\theta = \dfrac{360}{2\pi} = \dfrac{180}{\pi} \approx 57.3^\circ\), the same for every radius. This angle is called one radian.
