∞ Grade 9

For curious 14 and 15 year olds. The grade 9 course with the reasons left in, ideas from proof to algorithms that the course never reaches, and where the math shows up in the world. Three tracks: Sharpen (school math, and why it works), Around the corner (new ideas), Connections (where it shows up). One sheet a night, ten to fifteen minutes, and a starred problem if you want more.

Sharpen the grade 9 course, with the why

Factoring, quadratic equations, parabolas, surds, special triangles, trigonometric ratios, congruence, arcs and sectors, inverse variation, exponential functions, sequences, rearranging formulas, algebraic fractions, box plots, two-way tables and precision.

Sharpen 1

Un-multiplying: the sum and product hunt

To factor x² + 7x + 12, find two numbers that add to 7 and multiply to 12. A rectangle of tiles shows why those are the right two questions.
Sharpen 2

If a product is zero

Then one of the factors is. That single fact turns a quadratic equation into two easy linear ones, and it works for no other number.
Sharpen 3

The parabola's mirror line

Every parabola is symmetric. Find where it crosses the axis and the turning point sits exactly half way between.
Sharpen 4

Why √50 is 5√2

Pull the square factors out from under the root. Exact answers stay short, and √2 turns up everywhere.
Sharpen 5

Two triangles worth knowing by heart

Half a square and half an equilateral triangle. Their sides are 1, 1, √2 and 1, √3, 2, and they unlock exact answers for 30°, 45° and 60°.
Sharpen 6

Three ratios that measure an angle

In every right triangle with a 35° angle, opposite over hypotenuse is the same number. Give that number a name and you can measure heights you cannot reach.
Sharpen 7

Three facts fix a triangle (usually)

Three sides do. Two sides and the angle between them do. Two sides and a different angle may not, and a drawing shows the two triangles that fit.
Sharpen 8

A slice of a circle

An arc is a fraction of the circumference and a sector the same fraction of the area. The fraction is the angle out of 360.
Sharpen 9

When one doubles, the other halves

Speed and time for a fixed journey, pressure and volume, workers and days. The product stays constant and the graph is a curve that never touches the axes.
Sharpen 10

Adding versus multiplying

A linear function adds the same amount each step; an exponential one multiplies by the same amount. Tell them apart from a table, and write y = a·bˣ.
Sharpen 11

The nth term

Jump straight to the hundredth number of a sequence without writing the first ninety-nine. Constant difference gives a linear rule, constant ratio an exponential one.
Sharpen 12

Change the subject

A formula is an equation with several letters. Solve it for the one you want, once, and you never have to repeat the algebra.
Sharpen 13

Fractions with letters

They obey the same rules as fractions with numbers. Cancel common factors, find a common denominator, and never cancel across a plus sign.
Sharpen 14

Five numbers that draw a data set

Smallest, lower quartile, median, upper quartile, largest. A box plot shows centre, spread and skew at a glance, and flags outliers.
Sharpen 15

Two questions at once

Sort people by two things at the same time and a table shows whether the two are linked. Row percentages and column percentages answer different questions.
Sharpen 16

How precise is your answer?

A length given as 12 cm could be anything from 11.5 to 12.5. Carry the bounds through a calculation and see how many digits you can honestly keep.

Around the corner new ideas

Proof with letters, counterexamples, telescoping sums, the missing square, Catalan numbers, stars and bars, probability as area, Monte Carlo, timetabling, shortest paths, cheapest networks, the travelling salesman, stable matching, recursion, the Game of Life, logic gates, circles through three points, Heron's formula, great circles, the doughnut, the fifteen puzzle, game trees and the one-time pad.

Corner 1

Proof with letters

Call an odd number 2n + 1 and you can prove things about every odd number at once. The sum of two odds, the square of an odd, and a calendar trick.
Corner 2

One counterexample is enough

n² + n + 41 is prime for n = 0, 1, 2, … 39. Forty cases are not a proof, and n = 40 is why.
Corner 3

Why n³ − n always divides by 6

Factor it and you find three numbers in a row. One of any three in a row is a multiple of 3, and one of any two is even.
Corner 4

Sums that collapse

Write each term as a difference and the middle cancels, like a telescope closing. A sum of ninety-nine fractions becomes one subtraction.
Corner 5

The puzzle where 64 = 65

Cut an 8 by 8 square into four pieces and rearrange them into a 5 by 13 rectangle. The trick is a Fibonacci identity and a sliver you cannot see.
Corner 6

Counting balanced brackets

(()) and ()() are the two ways to balance two pairs. For three pairs there are 5, then 14, then 42. The same numbers count mountain ranges and handshakes across a table.
Corner 7

Stars and bars

How many ways can three children share ten identical sweets? Line up ten stars and two dividers and it is a counting problem you already know.
Corner 8

Probability you can measure with a ruler

Two friends each arrive at a random moment in the same hour and wait ten minutes. The chance they meet is an area.
Corner 9

Finding an area by throwing darts

Scatter random points over a square and count how many land under a curve. Luck, averaged, becomes a measurement.
Corner 10

Timetabling by colouring

Exams that share a student cannot share a slot. Draw the clashes as a graph, colour it, and the number of colours is the number of slots.
Corner 11

How a map app finds the shortest route

Dijkstra's method: always settle the nearest unsettled place next. Run it by hand on a small map and see why it never has to go back.
Corner 12

The cheapest way to connect everything

Join towns with cable using as little as possible. Being greedy, always adding the cheapest safe link, gives the best answer every time.
Corner 13

The problem nobody can solve quickly

Visit every town once and come home by the shortest route. The greedy idea that built the cheapest network fails here, and no fast method is known.
Corner 14

Matching without regrets

Pair applicants with schools so that no applicant and school would both rather have each other. A simple round of proposals always finds such a matching.
Corner 15

How many ways up the stairs?

Take one step or two at a time. The count for ten stairs comes from the counts for nine and eight, and a familiar sequence appears.
Corner 16

A universe with four rules

Cells on a grid live or die by counting neighbours. From that come still lifes, blinkers, and a glider that walks.
Corner 17

Adding with switches

AND, OR and NOT are enough to build a circuit that adds. Wire a half adder on paper and see how a chip does arithmetic.
Corner 18

The circle through three points

Any three points not in a line lie on exactly one circle. Its centre is where two perpendicular bisectors cross, and the third always passes through the same spot.
Corner 19

Area from three sides

No height needed. Heron's formula gives the area of a triangle from its sides alone, and you can check it against one you already know.
Corner 20

Why planes fly over Greenland

On a globe the shortest path is part of a great circle, and on a flat map it looks bent. A string on a ball shows it.
Corner 21

A doughnut is not a ball

Corners minus edges plus faces is 2 for anything shaped like a ball. For a doughnut it is 0, and that number cannot be changed by stretching.
Corner 22

The puzzle that cannot be solved

Swap two tiles of the fifteen puzzle and no amount of sliding will fix it. A quantity that every move leaves unchanged proves it.
Corner 23

Playing noughts and crosses perfectly

Draw every possible game as a tree, score the endings, and work backwards. With best play the game is always a draw.
Corner 24

The unbreakable code

Add a random key, bit by bit, and the message could be anything. Reuse the key once and it falls apart.

Connections money · medicine · bridges · navigation · building · fuel · population · cameras

The same math, found in the wild.

Connections 1

How long until your money doubles?

Divide 72 by the interest rate. At 6% it takes about 12 years. Where the rule comes from, and how well it works.
Connections 2

How long does a medicine last?

Half the dose is gone after one half-life, three quarters after two. Repeated doses climb towards a steady level you can calculate.
Connections 3

The shape of a bridge

A suspension bridge's cable hangs in a parabola. Fit y = ax² to a span and work out the length of every hanger.
Connections 4

Finding your way with angles

A bearing is an angle clockwise from north. Two legs of a journey, a right triangle, and trigonometry tells you how far you are from home.
Connections 5

How steep is too steep?

A wheelchair ramp may rise 1 in 12. A roof pitch, a road sign's 10% and a staircase are all the same idea, and tangent converts between them.
Connections 6

The miles-per-gallon illusion

Swapping a 10 mpg car for a 20 mpg one saves far more fuel than swapping 30 for 60. Inverse variation fools almost everyone.
Connections 7

Will food keep up with people?

In 1798 Malthus argued that population multiplies while food only adds. Compare an exponential with a line, find where they cross, and see what he missed.
Connections 8

Why camera settings go 2, 2.8, 4, 5.6

Each step halves the light. The numbers are powers of √2, because the light depends on the area of the opening.
