Why radians

Measure an angle by the arc it cuts off and arc length is just radius times angle. Degrees are a habit; radians are what the circle itself uses.

Name ____________________Date ____________

Tonight's idea

The number 360 was chosen by people. The circle knows nothing about it.

Draw an angle at the centre of a circle of radius \(r\). It cuts off an arc of length \(s\). Double the radius and the arc doubles, so the ratio \(s/r\) depends only on the angle. Use that ratio as the angle.

The angle in radians is \(\theta = \dfrac{s}{r}\): arc length divided by radius. One radian cuts off an arc exactly as long as the radius.

The whole circumference is \(2\pi r\), so a full turn is \(2\pi r / r = 2\pi\) radians, about 6.28. Hence

\[180^\circ = \pi \text{ radians}, \qquad 1 \text{ radian} = \frac{180^\circ}{\pi} \approx 57.3^\circ.\]

Turn the definition round and you get arc length. Take the fraction \(\theta/2\pi\) of the disc's area \(\pi r^2\) and you get the area of a sector (a slice):

\(s = r\theta\) and \(A = \tfrac12 r^2\theta\), with \(\theta\) in radians.

In degrees both need a factor of \(\pi/180\), as in grade 9.

Watch one: a slice of pizza

A pizza of radius 15 cm is cut into slices with an angle of \(40^\circ\) at the tip. Convert first: multiply by \(\pi/180\).

\[\theta = 40 \times \frac{\pi}{180} = \frac{2\pi}{9} \approx 0.698.\]

The crust is the arc: \(s = r\theta = 15 \times \tfrac{2\pi}{9} = \tfrac{10\pi}{3} \approx 10.5\) cm. The area is \(A = \tfrac12 \times 15^2 \times \tfrac{2\pi}{9} = 25\pi \approx 78.5\) cm². Check: \(40^\circ\) is one ninth of a turn, and one ninth of \(\pi \times 15^2\) is \(25\pi\).

Drag a point round the circle and watch the angle in radians: infinityclub.net/demos/unitcircle.html.

Your turn

  1. Write \(60^\circ\) and \(135^\circ\) in radians, as multiples of \(\pi\). Write \(\tfrac{5\pi}{6}\) radians and 2 radians in degrees.
  2. A sector has radius 6 cm and angle \(\tfrac{2\pi}{3}\). Find its arc length and its area. Leave \(\pi\) in your answers.
  3. A bicycle wheel has radius 0.35 m. It turns through 20 radians without slipping. How far does the bicycle move? Roughly how many full turns is that?
  4. Seen from Earth, the Moon fills an angle of about 0.009 radians, and it is about 384,000 km away. Estimate the Moon's diameter. Your method treats a straight line as if it were an arc. Why is that safe here, and when would it fail?

Stretch: the best slice

A 20 cm piece of string is bent into the outline of a sector: two radii and an arc. Write the area in terms of \(r\) alone and complete the square. Which radius gives the largest area, and what is the angle then?

Answers

  1. \(60^\circ = \tfrac{\pi}{3}\) and \(135^\circ = \tfrac{3\pi}{4}\). \(\tfrac{5\pi}{6} = 150^\circ\) and 2 radians \(= \tfrac{360^\circ}{\pi} \approx 114.6^\circ\).
  2. \(s = r\theta = 6 \times \tfrac{2\pi}{3} = 4\pi\) cm, about 12.6 cm. \(A = \tfrac12 \times 36 \times \tfrac{2\pi}{3} = 12\pi\) cm², about 37.7 cm².
  3. The ground covered equals the arc that has rolled past: \(s = r\theta = 0.35 \times 20 = 7\) m. One turn is \(2\pi\) radians, so that is \(20 / 2\pi \approx 3.2\) turns.
  4. \(s = r\theta = 384{,}000 \times 0.009 = 3{,}456\) km, so about 3,500 km (the measured value is about 3,470 km). The diameter is a straight chord, not an arc, but for a small angle the chord and the arc are almost the same length. It would fail for a large angle: at \(\pi\) radians the arc is \(\pi r\) while the chord is only \(2r\).

Stretch. The outline is \(2r + r\theta = 20\), so \(r\theta = 20 - 2r\). Then \(A = \tfrac12 r^2\theta = \tfrac12 r(20 - 2r) = 10r - r^2 = 25 - (r - 5)^2\). The largest area is 25 cm², at \(r = 5\) cm, and then \(\theta = 10/5 = 2\) radians. The answer is 2 radians for any length of string: the best sector has an arc as long as its two radii together.
